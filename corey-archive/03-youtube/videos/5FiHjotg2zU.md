Key Points

Karpathy's "idea file" breaks the second brain into three tiers — raw (brain dump), wiki (AI-organized knowledge), and outputs (query results) — and Nick built a full Claude skill around this exact structure.

Nick's free skill is agent-agnostic: it works with Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, OpenCode, and more, and installs via a guided setup wizard directly from a GitHub repo.

You can automate the ingest step using Claude Code's loop feature so your vault indexes new raw content on a schedule — no manual intervention needed.

The second brain compounds in value over time — on day zero it's limited, but after 30, 60, or 90 days of consistent input it becomes a private knowledge moat that no competitor can replicate.

The Lint command health-checks your wiki for outdated entries and gaps, then suggests exactly what raw content to add next to close those gaps.

Section Summaries

1. The Second Brain Framework
I bring on Nick to break down Karpathy's viral tweet and the "idea file" concept behind it. Nick explains how he translated that loosely defined framework into a complete Claude skill structured around three tiers: raw input, a wiki, and outputs — and why this architecture matters.
