Summaries

1. The Valuation Math: How Software Apps Get Priced for Acquisition
The software acquisition market follows predictable multiples. Apps typically sell for 3-5X their annual recurring revenue. Ritesh's app generated roughly $70K in trailing 12-month revenue, making it worth $210K-$350K at market rates. But some apps command even higher multiples. One example: a restaurant SaaS earning just $800/month sold for $65K because the buyer valued the customer data. If your app delivers value while collecting valuable data, you've built the two most important factors for maximizing acquisition price. The playbook is almost like software dropshipping—build something that generates recurring revenue, let it run with minimal maintenance costs ($200/month for support), then sell for a multiple of annual earnings.

2. Finding a Niche Problem and Marketing Through Communities
Ritesh's app helps restaurant servers automatically book shifts. In certain states, servers must manually claim shifts through apps, requiring them to constantly watch their phones. The AI app automates this process—servers set their preferences and the app books shifts for them. Marketing didn't require paid ads or complex funnels. Ritesh found his customers in niche communities where servers already congregated: Reddit threads and Facebook groups dedicated to restaurant workers discussing shift-booking frustrations. He engaged authentically, demonstrated the solution, and converted users to paying customers. At peak, the app had only 15-16 customers paying monthly subscriptions—enough to generate $7-8K monthly and attract acquisition interest.

3. The Acquisition Process: From Listing to $200K Offer
Acquire.com handles the entire acquisition workflow. Ritesh created a listing that included Stripe revenue screenshots, a P&L statement showing 12 months of revenue and costs, an intro video showcasing the app, screenshots of the interface, and a marketing plan showing buyers how to scale further. He initially listed at $200K, lowered to $165K to generate more buyer calls, then received a $200K offer anyway. The buyer chose seller financing: $30K upfront plus $170K paid over six months. Financing deals typically command higher prices because buyers preserve cash while sellers get premium valuations. The platform handles escrow, agreements, and payment facilitation for approximately 4% fee—eliminating the risk of shady deals or broken agreements.
