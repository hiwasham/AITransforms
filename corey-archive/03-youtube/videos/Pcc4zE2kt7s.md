Key Points

The QuickBooks analogy destroys the most common objection: prospects aren't paying for access to AI tools, they're paying for someone who knows what to do with them.

Skeptics don't need more hype — start with one concrete win they can see working in days so they judge on results, not the latest tool.

"Too expensive" is the easiest objection to flip: if a few thousand dollars buys back five hours a week, the expensive option is doing nothing.

Owners who got bad results with AI jumped straight into tinkering instead of auditing their workflows with an effort versus impact matrix first.

No business is too specialized — every business runs on the same five pillars: sales, marketing, finance, operations, and intelligence.

Position AI as automating tasks, not roles — it pulls grunt work off the team so they can do the job they were hired for.

"Let me think about it" gets reframed around compounding cost: every week of waiting is more hours lost to a task a $999 assessment could have flagged for automation.
