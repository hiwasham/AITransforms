Key Points

• Every prescription has to pull one of three ROI levers, effectiveness (more revenue), efficiency (hours back), or quality (happier customers), and you weight it to the lever the owner picks.

• Meeting one is pure discovery: prescribe nothing, ask five questions, and close by naming the single highest-opportunity bottleneck back to them.

• The ROI anchor gets the owner to quantify the pain themselves. Two hours a week at $200 an hour is $400 a week the automation gives back.

• The bottleneck worth fixing sits at the intersection of high frequency and high friction.

• The prescription tree: common tasks get an off-the-shelf tool, judgment, writing, or research tasks get Claude Cowork, and repeatable workflows unique to the business get a custom Claude skill.

• When prescribing a custom skill, tell them the what, not the how. That gap is the upsell.

• The money question (hand it off, build it with you, or build it for you) converts 30 to 50% of free assessments into paid clients.
