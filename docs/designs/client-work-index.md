## Problem Statement

AITransforms (Hiwa) has produced **155 files / 7.1 MB** of Bayan client work in
`marketing-plans/bayan`: a trilingual marketing plan, GEO fixes, 10 Prometric pages,
Days 31–60 content, an analytics event spec, CEO decision briefs, a Product Brain
proposal, a B2B track + sizing memo, a sales diagnostic, a growth-engine design, and a
71-file product archive. The only delivery channel is **email + one attachment**. The
CEO (Dr. Abdullah Al-Alawi) therefore sees a fraction of the work; the depth, the
system, and the organization are invisible. Preparing each send costs Hiwa real time
and energy (deciding what to send, finding the files).

The goal is *not* to show the CEO everything. It is to give a low-touch, outcomes-first
founder an **opt-in way to see the depth if curiosity strikes**, so that (a) the
relationship reads as *a system*, not a freelancer's attachments, (b) a curious CEO can
browse without asking, and (c) the next project is more likely to come to AITransforms.
Hiwa's own framing: *"if we can impress him we can get the other projects too. if not,
maybe we disconnect and he works with others."*

## Demand Evidence

**Honest read: this is a retention/expansion play, not a demand-fulfilling one — and
that is fine as long as we design for the real demand signal.**

- The CEO has **not** explicitly asked to browse the work. He reacts to *outcomes*.
  The shared-language doc records his style verbatim: *"I care about the outcome rather
  than number of posts,"* *"I share with you objectives and you have freedom to build on
  them. I don't like micromanaging things,"* and his real pain is *coordination cycles*
  (*"This is taking a lot of my time honestly"*).
- Hiwa's own words on the demand: *"he asked implicitly. i just wanna let him see more
  if he want for more. so if he was curious he can see without asking."*
- **The real demand signal is the relationship viability bet:** a founder who trusts you
  forwards your work upward. Everything in this design serves *making a forward
  possible and safe*, not driving browsing volume.
- Supporting evidence that depth matters at all: the CEO runs another AI engineer on a
  low-touch cadence and evaluates output, not activity. The index must therefore be
  **outcome-led, not artifact-led** — the single most important design constraint, and
  the one most exposed to being violated.

## Status Quo

Today, preparing a send to the CEO:
- Hiwa decides what to include by memory, then hunts through 155 files to assemble it.
- The CEO receives a single attachment; the rest of the work is invisible.
- There is no index, no status ("drafted / ready / sent / approved"), no single entry
  point. Hiwa, in his own words, carries the map in his head.
- Cost: prep time + the recurring, unquantified risk that the CEO under-values the work
  because he only ever sees one file at a time.

**Bottleneck classification (from Q2):** the immediate pain is the **sharing step**
(pack + send, repeatedly, with no reusable system). The deeper pains — being the index,
and deciding what's ready — are real and acknowledged, and are routed to `TODOS.md` as
the roadmap, not solved in v1.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target reader:** the CEO plus his inner circle (Nasim as co-operator; possibly the
other AI engineer or an exec he forwards to). Design requirement: **every section must
stand alone and survive a forward** — no dependence on Hiwa narrating it.

**The forwarding reader's risk:** a person who forwards your work upward is putting
their own credibility on the line. The index must make *them* look good.

**Narrowest wedge:** **a single private link to one page** that opens with "what changed
for Bayan" and lists every artifact, grouped and one-line-summarized, with links to the
real files. No login, no portal, no per-section access. Ships in half a day.

## Constraints

- **Privacy is absolute.** `marketing-plans/bayan` contains real client data (the real
  domain `bayan.edu.om`, real figures, the plan, the CEO's name). The index must not
  leak this beyond a private, unguessable URL shared with the CEO. This is the opposite
  posture from the *public* anonymized `/work` portfolio on AITransforms' own site.
- **No CMS / DB / auth / backend.** Static files only, per the AITransforms site build
  packet constraints and the "no heavy dependencies" rule.
- **Reuse, don't rebuild.** Existing HTML artifacts already rendered —
  `marketing plan.html`, `product marketing.html`, `session-review.html` — should be
  linked as pages, not re-authored.
- **Low-touch.** The CEO's scarcest resource is his attention; the page must be scannable
  in under 90 seconds and reward a deeper read only if he chooses.
- **Trilingual is NOT required for this artifact.** This is a private client deliverable
  read by an English-first CEO (his market's medical study is in English; the shared-
  language doc fixes English-first). Unlike the public site, this index is English-only
  unless the CEO asks otherwise. (Explicitly contrasted with the prior `/work` design,
  where trilingual was mandatory.)

## Premises

- **P1 — Outcome > volume.** The index leads with what changed (numbers, problems fixed),
  not "look how many files I made." *Agreed.* A 155-file gallery that leads with volume
  reads as busywork to a founder who "cares about outcomes, not number of posts."
- **P2 — Forwardable, no login.** A single unguessable URL shared with the CEO + inner
  circle is the whole access model for v1. Real auth is over-engineering. *Agreed.*
- **P3 — It fixes Hiwa's prep time.** A living index means "what do I send" becomes a
  two-minute pick from a maintained page, not a 90-minute hunt. *Agreed.*
- **P4 — Single source of truth.** The page is generated from one maintained manifest, so
  it updates in minutes and never drifts from reality. *Agreed.* This also makes the
  artifact a live demonstration of the exact drift-discipline thesis the Product Brain
  proposal sells to Bayan. **The manifest IS the pitch.**

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Static "Work Index" page (one hand-authored HTML file, one link)
A single `index.html` listing every artifact by category, one line each. Effort S,
Risk Low. Fastest to a link. **Rejected as primary:** a flat list drifts toward volume,
violating P1; local file links break when shared unless the artifacts are also hosted.

### Approach B: Manifest-generated index (`MANIFEST.md` → HTML), private URL  — CHOSEN
One `bayan-manifest.md` (single source of truth: `path`, `title`, `category`, `status`,
`one_line`, `outcome`) that a small generator turns into a clean HTML index. The page
opens with the **"What changed for Bayan" outcome strip**, then the categorized index.
Hosted on a private URL (GitHub Pages on a private repo, or Vercel under a random
subdomain). Effort M, Risk Low–Med. Satisfies P1–P4 at once and doubles as proof of the
source-of-truth discipline.

### Approach C: Notion / Google Drive index
Build the index in Notion (or a Drive tree) and share one link. Effort S, Risk Low.
Fastest and editable in seconds, a format the CEO's world already uses. **Rejected as
primary:** it reads as "a Notion page," not an AITransforms system, weakening the
central "we build systems" claim; and it inserts a third-party account into the middle
of client work.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B — the manifest-generated Work Index.** Hiwa chose it over the office-hours
recommendation's faster fallback (A) because it is the only option that satisfies all
four premises *and* turns the deliverable itself into evidence of the method AITransforms
sells.

**Shape:**

1. **`marketing-plans/bayan/bayan-manifest.md`** — the single source of truth. A table,
   one row per artifact: `path` · `title` · `category` · `status` · `one_line` ·
   `outcome`. Status vocabulary: `drafted` / `ready` / `sent` / `approved`. This file is
   the thing Hiwa edits; everything else is generated.
2. **`marketing-plans/bayan/portal/index.html`** — generated, self-contained, no external
   deps. Section order, top to bottom:
   - **Outcome strip** (P1): 3–5 "what changed" items (e.g. the live drift report's three
     conflicts; the fixed JournalReady pricing). Outcomes first, artifacts second.
   - **Grouped index**: categories (Marketing Plan · Content · Technical/GEO · Strategy &
     Decisions · Product Archive), each row = title + one-line "why it matters" + link.
   - **The method note**: one short paragraph making the manifest/source-of-truth point
     explicitly ("this index is generated from one file — the same drift-discipline
     we're proposing for Bayan").
   - **Footer**: contact + a one-line "request anything → single line" ask.
3. **`marketing-plans/bayan/portal/build.*`** — a tiny generator (`build.mjs` or a shell
   `awk`/`jq` one-shot) that reads the manifest and writes `index.html`. No framework, no
   npm install beyond what the repo already has.
4. **Artifacts as pages**: link the manifest rows to hosted copies. Simplest viable:
   publish the whole `bayan/portal/` folder (index + copies of the key HTML artifacts +
   Markdown rendered to HTML) to a private static host. `.md` files can be linked as
   `.md` or rendered by the generator into minimal HTML.
5. **Hosting + privacy:** GitHub Pages on a **private** repo, or Vercel under a random
   subdomain (`bayan-work-a1b2c3.vercel.app`). The URL is the secret. Confirm which host
   before build. (If neither is wanted, the fallback is: open the local `index.html` and
   send a PDF export — weaker, but zero hosting.)

**Anti-drift guardrail:** the index is generated, never hand-edited. If a fact in the
index must change, it changes in the manifest and regenerates. This keeps the artifact
honest and is the live demo of the thesis.

## Open Questions

1. **Hosting choice** (blocks publishing): GitHub Pages private repo vs Vercel random
   subdomain vs local+PDF. Affects the link permanence and the "is the URL private
   enough" call.
2. **How much to render vs link:** do we render each `.md` to HTML in the index, or link
   raw files? Rendering is more impressive; linking is faster and always current. (Rec:
   render the *headline* artifacts — marketing plan, product marketing, session review —
   and link the rest.)
3. **The outcome strip's content** (P1, critical path): which 3–5 outcomes go on top?
   Must be ones Hiwa will defend verbatim (same bar as the proposal's claims gate).
4. **When to first send the link:** tied to the next CEO touchpoint. It should ride
   *with* a real send ("here's the plan — and everything behind it is here if you want
   it"), not arrive as a naked link.

## Success Criteria

- Hiwa can send a single link (private, no login) that opens with Bayan outcomes and
  lists the work grouped and summarized — **and he can update it in under 5 minutes** by
  editing one manifest file and regenerating.
- The page reads in one scroll as "this person runs a system," not "this person sent me
  a file."
- Client data is reachable only by the intended recipients: password-protected host +
  unguessable URL + `noindex`/`robots.txt`; no search-engine exposure; a documented
  revocation path (rotate password / redeploy) exists for the case where access must be
  pulled.
- The index itself demonstrates single-source-of-truth discipline (generated from one
  manifest; a fact lives in exactly one place).
- It survives a forward: each row is self-explanatory; the outcome strip stands alone.

## Distribution Plan

Static site generated from the manifest, published to a private static host:
- **Option 1 (rec):** GitHub Pages on a private repo (link is unguessable; repo private).
- **Option 2:** Vercel, random subdomain.
- **Option 3 (fallback):** local `index.html` → PDF, attached to email.
No CI required for v1; a manual `node build.mjs` (or `npm run` alias) regenerates and
the host auto-deploys on push. If this proves useful across multiple clients, it becomes
the reusable "client work index" template (roadmap, see `TODOS.md`).

## Dependencies

- **Manifest authoring:** one pass to tag all 155 files with category + status + one-line

- **Hosting decision** (Open Q1).
- **Reuse-ready:** the existing HTML artifacts; the static-dir discipline from the prior

## The Assignment

**Before building anything:** write the **outcome strip** — the 3–5 "what changed for
Bayan" items that go at the very top of the index — each with the number and the source,
at the same evidence bar as a live sales claim. This is the critical path (it is P1, the
premise most likely to decide whether the CEO reads on) and it forces the honest answer
to "what has this work actually changed?" A concrete action, not "go build it": draft the
strip in `bayan-manifest.md` today, and if you cannot fill a defensible outcome for a
category, that tells you where the work has *volume without result* — the exact thing a
low-touch founder would notice.
