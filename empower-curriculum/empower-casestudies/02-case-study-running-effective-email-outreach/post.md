**The Journey: An Operations Guy Discovers AI**

Mick spent 13 years in the call center industry in the Philippines, primarily focused on operations. His job was to put up call center sites to support the U.S. market. Operations was his domain—he understood the workflows, the bottlenecks, the daily rhythm of getting work done. But he knew nothing about IT, coding, or any of the technical stuff.

Like many operations professionals, Mick saw opportunities and gaps everyday at Acquira that needed addressing. The problem was that figuring out solutions and implementing them took significant time. That changed when Hayden, Acquira’s CEO, started playing around with AI.

When Hayden mentioned he was experimenting with AI, Mick asked for access and began his own exploration. A few weeks later, the beta cohort for Second Brain launched, and Mick joined. That’s where the pieces of the puzzle came together—where he understood how to use this tool to benefit himself, his team, and everyone around him.

## **Setting Up Claude: The Master Prompt**

Mick’s approach to Claude started with building his own master prompt—a comprehensive set of instructions that lives in his user settings. This master prompt focuses on who Acquira is, what he does, and the company’s core values. It includes everything: the products they sell, their thought process, and how they want to approach things.

Building this might seem overwhelming at first, Mick acknowledges. But if you’ve gone through the cohort, piece by piece you can put this together and get to a master prompt of your own that’s designed specifically to what you do and what’s important for you.

The master prompt works at the macro level—it covers Mick’s overall role and contribution to the company. But for specific projects, he creates dedicated Claude Projects with their own instructions and artifacts. These projects work at the micro level, very specific to certain tasks he wants Claude to accomplish. User settings define the big picture while projects focus on specific tasks.

**The Reality: Explaining Email Campaigns to Prospects**

The trigger for this particular project came from a fundamental sales challenge. Acquira’s sales team needed help explaining their off-market outreach product to prospective clients—entrepreneurs looking to acquire businesses.

It’s very difficult for a salesperson to sell something that can’t be visualized by the prospect. When Nathan, one of Acquira’s salespeople, would jump on a call and mention they’d be sending 25,000 emails, prospects had questions: What do you show for it? How does it work? When does it start?

But the need went beyond just sales. Mick also recognized that the team needed something to keep them on track—a way to forecast the level of results clients would get from Acquira. Business owners need to plan their time: Can I attend these two meetings? Do I have capacity to qualify three leads at this point?

For Mick, there was another frustration. It’s hard to explain from one department to another what you’re doing and how you can align as one in terms of offering this as a product. Both the sales team, the internal operations team needed to know when calls would be booked, when meetings would happen, and what volume of work to expect.

**The Recipe: Building the Solution**

## **Step 1: Creating the Project with Instructions and Artifacts**

To solve this problem, Mick created a dedicated Claude Project for email campaigns. The first step was critical: Claude needed a perfect understanding of what he was trying to do.

Your AI needs to know exactly the space where you’re coming from and your perspective on how to get things done. Without that context, you’re just getting generic responses.

He created specific instructions for the campaign work Acquira does, including the level of knowledge he had within the email outreach space (he was fairly new to it), and saved artifacts from conversations he was having day to day with Claude.

He had Claude create the instructions outside the project. Then he copied and pasted those instructions into the specific project. He also uploaded artifacts—saved pieces of content from previous conversations that would give Claude context.

## **Step 2: The Conversation—Challenge Everything**

With the project set up, Mick began his conversation with Claude. The key is to truly engage with it, he says, and never be afraid to challenge what it gives you. This is where many users go wrong. They ask AI chatbots like Claude a question, get an answer, and accept it without pushing back. As Mick puts it, that’s as bad as going to Google and trying to find medication for a random rash you have.

The conversation started with a simple question: What is the recommended sent count for emails that were just purchased from Mailforge—new domain, new emails? What would be their capacity?

This question came from Mick being very new to the email outreach space. Acquira was working on this, had purchased list data from Mailforge, and needed to understand the technical constraints.

Claude started asking clarifying questions back. It presented what it was seeing from industry standards, and Mick engaged in a back-and-forth conversation, providing specific information. For Mailforge, they only allow five email accounts per domain.

Mick approaches these conversations as if he’s extracting information from a colleague. This project would be his mentor in the email outreach space.

As the conversation progressed, Claude created a first iteration of the calculator. It had some basic functionality—counting leads and domains with a slider interface. But a lot of things didn’t actually work. It looked pretty but didn’t function properly.

So Mick challenged it. He asked for specific changes, like adding a percentage sign. He asked questions when he didn’t know something, like how to share this with others. Claude suggested options: CodePen, Replit, Vercel, Netlify.

Mick remembered that on a separate project, he had created a landing page for Acquira—generated the HTML code inside Claude and sent it to the IT department to upload. This experience gave him the understanding that he could upload code somewhere and it could look the same.

Through continued iteration, Mick refined what he was seeing. He estimates around 2 hours of sitting down to have this conversation, and it would resolve long-term problems for you and the teams that you work with.

## **Step 3: Choosing Netlify as the Hosting Platform**

When it came time to decide on a hosting platform, Mick evaluated all the options Claude provided. He chose Netlify for a simple reason: it was the most user-friendly and it was free.

Netlify gives you the option to just click from your folder and drag it inside your browser, and it creates everything for you as long as you have the right code. Mick had no previous experience with Netlify—he reviewed each option Claude recommended and landed on this decision. Going through Claude’s five options was easier than doing the research from scratch.

## **Step 4: Creating a Generic Version**

Once Mick had a working version for Acquira’s internal use, he realized something important. This tool could be useful for different types of campaigns beyond just Acquira’s specific use case. He didn’t want Acquira branding on it because it might be used for different types of campaigns.

Mick asked Claude to make it shareable. Claude’s recommendation was straightforward: go with a new chat and tell it to recreate the tool.

Why a new chat? If he stayed in his project, Claude would apply all the context from either his master prompt or his project prompt—including Acquira’s branding guidelines and communication style.

So Mick started a fresh conversation outside the project with a simple instruction: recreate this tool and make it as generic as possible.

Through more iterations to ensure it would work for different types of people running different campaigns, Mick created a Version 2. He downloaded it as an HTML file, went to Netlify, uploaded the file, and the tool was live.

## **Understanding What the Tool Does**

The email campaign calculator addresses several critical aspects of running an effective email outreach campaign.

**Domain and Email Account Planning**

It calculates how many domains you need and how many email accounts you need based on your campaign goals.

**Lead Targeting**

You can specify how many leads you’re targeting and see whether that’s enough. Having that foresight is very important.

**Conversion Funnel Assumptions**

The calculator includes conversion funnel assumptions that can be customized. If you’re a business owner with your own funnel numbers, you can input them and see how they would yield results. If you don’t have funnel metrics, you can ask Claude to provide industry benchmarks to start with.

When Mick tested this with Claude, asking for typical conversion rates in the M&A industry, Claude’s estimates were remarkably accurate—right within the range of Acquira’s actual performance at 0.64%.

**Campaign Priority Split**

One feature Mick finds particularly useful is the campaign priority split. Many people think about email campaigns as simply blasting emails out. What they don’t understand is that the same email accounts you’re using need to serve multiple purposes—reaching out to new leads and following up on previous leads. This definitely affects conversion numbers.

The slider in the tool lets you allocate your email account capacity: How many new emails do you want to send? How many follow-ups?

**Week-by-Week Warm-Up Progression**

The tool accounts for the warm-up process automatically. It shows a focus on warming up in the first week, partial warm-up in the second week, and so on until you reach full capacity—while still maintaining some warm-up activity.

The output shows:

- How many leads per day you’re reaching out to
- How many days it will take to complete the campaign
- How many weeks to complete
- Total emails you’re looking to send

**A Note on Email Warm-Up**

For those unfamiliar with email marketing, the warm-up process is critical. Many entrepreneurs don’t understand why their emails land in spam. The issue is email warm-up—or the lack of it.

Think of email servers like a gate to a house, Mick explains. If you don’t have a good reputation within the community, you won’t be allowed to get through the gate.

Email warm-up is a process where your email account builds reputation by sending and receiving emails from within your network. During the warm-up phase, if you have ten email accounts, they send and receive emails among themselves. This establishes reputation with email servers so your accounts are trusted and messages get through.

If you start blasting emails on the very first day you get your email account, everything goes to spam. The email servers don’t know you—why would they let you in?

Beyond warm-up, there’s the question of acting normal. What you want from your email accounts is for them to behave as if an actual person is using that email sender account. You’re not going to send 500 emails on your first day with a new work email. You’d probably send 5.

The tool replicates this natural progression. It shows you how many emails you should send week by week based on the number of email sender accounts you have.

**The Results: Visualization Changes Everything**

The tool has been implemented for about a week at the time of this interview, but early results are already visible.

**For the Sales Team**

The calculator has generated more interest in Acquira’s buyer pool. When Nathan jumps on a call with prospects, it’s no longer vague. Instead of just saying they’re sending 25,000 emails, he can show them exactly how it works using the tool.

This has generated interest within the community. Prospects who talk to Nathan can see specifically how the campaign would look for them as business owners working on the businesses endorsed to them. They can check their calendars and realistically plan: Can I attend these two meetings? Can I qualify three leads at this point in time?

**For Internal Teams**

Internally, the biggest win is the level of understanding it creates across departments. The sales team and internal operations team can now see when they’ll be needed to book calls, when they’ll need to jump on meetings, and how much volume to expect.

There’s a forecast now on what’s about to happen. That visibility and alignment across teams—that’s the real value.

**For Business Owners and Entrepreneurs**

For entrepreneurs looking to implement email marketing campaigns on their own, the tool provides the ability to understand what type of email accounts they’ll need and how much volume they’ll be sending over time.

This kind of planning saves money because you’ll understand exactly how many leads you’re sending and what the capacity requirements are. It gives you the ability to plan ahead.

**Time and Cost Investment**

The total investment? A Claude subscription, a Netlify subscription (free tier), and about 2 hours of Mick’s time to build the initial tool.

The result has been the ability to conserve both time and resources across multiple teams.

**Beyond Small Business: Enterprise Applications**

While this case study focuses on how the tool applies in an entrepreneurial, unorganized, small business environment, the implications extend far beyond that context.

Individual teams and marketing teams within large organizations could use this same tool and approach. Even mature organizations with sophisticated marketing functions might not have this level of insight today—or if they do, they’re likely paying significant costs to outside providers for it. Even with the best enterprise tools that exist in the marketplace, this kind of forecasting capability might still be out of reach or prohibitively expensive.

Anyone seeking to become AI-enabled in an organization, large or small, can follow a similar sequence. The accessibility is what’s striking. The cost is essentially a Claude subscription and some hours of focused work. As one person working on a team, you can create tools that provide enterprise-level insights without enterprise-level costs.

## **Final Thoughts**

Going back to his roots in operations, Mick concludes that this approach is going to be extremely helpful to whoever has the guts to spend time with it and play around with it.

For operations professionals, for salespeople, for team leaders in large organizations, for entrepreneurs—the message is the same. The tools are accessible. The cost is minimal. What’s required is the willingness to invest the time, engage in the conversation, and iterate until you have something that works.

In Mick’s case, around 2 hours of conversation with Claude created a tool that’s now being used across sales, operations, and client-facing contexts. It’s providing visibility where there was vagueness, creating alignment where there was confusion, and enabling better planning where there was uncertainty.

That’s the promise of becoming AI-enabled: not replacing human expertise, but augmenting it, visualizing it, and making it accessible to everyone who needs it.
