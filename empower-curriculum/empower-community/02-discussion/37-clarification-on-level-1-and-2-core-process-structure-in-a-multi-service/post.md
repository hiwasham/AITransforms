# Clarification on Level 1 & 2 Core Process Structure in a Multi-Service Agency

> Discussion › Post 37 › by Yujin Lee › 2026-02-23

[Open on Circle](https://community.empowerlabs.ai/c/discussion-b92449/clarification-on-level-1-2-core-process-structure-in-a-multi-service-agency)

## Content

**Core Process:**  
Sale to Delivery

**Challenge:**  
I’m unsure how to properly structure my Level 1 and Level 2 SOPs because my agency offers multiple services (both one-off and retainer-based), and clients often engage us for a mix of them depending on the goals.

Our services include:

- Branding (one-off)
- Website Creation (one-off)
- SEO (retainer)
- SEM (retainer)
- Social Media Marketing (retainer)
- Strategy Workshop (one-off)
- Influencer Marketing (retainer)

I’m unclear whether I should:

1. Create **one overarching Level 1 “Sale to Delivery” core process** that applies to the entire agency, and then develop Level 2 blueprints for each service,

or

1. Create **separate Level 1 & 2 SOPs for each service** individually.

I’m also uncertain about Exercise 3.6 (Register Your Organization), which states to name the process:

> Sale to Delivery – Company Name – Product/Service Name

Does this imply we are expected to create and upload a separate core process into Stage 3 for each individual service?

**What I’ve tried:**  
I’ve mapped the high-level Sale to Delivery flow for the company as a whole, but I can see how each service has its own unique delivery mechanics. That’s where I’m struggling to determine the correct structural level.

**What kind of input I’m looking for:**  
I’d love guidance on the *correct structural approach* within the EMPOWER framework.  
Specifically:

- Is Level 1 typically company-wide or service-specific?
- How granular should we go when registering core processes in Stage 3?

Appreciate any clarity from those further along in the implementation. Thank you!

  Hi @mention Hi Yujin,

  I believe the recommendation is to focus on one core process now to avoid becoming overwhelmed. However, in the long run, if you have distinct customer journeys for each service, creating a core process for each product line may make sense. Have you considered grouping the one-off and retainer services? They might share enough similarities to be categorized as two core service types that encompass all your offerings.

  Although the services we provide are very different, I related to your point about having both retainer and one-off clients. My commercial landscaping business engages with customers similarly as our work falls into two primary models: (1) recurring monthly maintenance contracts (similar to your retainer clients) and (2) one-off enhancement or project-based services (similar to your one-off clients).

  For this cohort I’m focusing on just one core process: recurring maintenance contracts.  Although we offer many types of enhancement or project-based work, we consider the customer journey similar enough to group into one core process. Therefore, even though we offer many different services, we will limit our core processes to just two.

- **Josh Cordes** · 2026-02-24
  Mike is correct here @mention.

  In the Session 3 assessment prompt, it states:

  *"My business may have multiple products or services, and I should go through this questionnaire for each one. For this assessment, let's focus on the one that brings my company the highest revenue."*

  And the Level 1 example naming convention in the 4-Level Documentation Framework shows:

  *"Sale to Delivery – Product 1"*

  So start with one service, the one generating the highest revenue. Run the assessment for that service, then build the Level 1 and Level 2 SOPs for it. Don't try to map all seven services at once.

  You can absolutely come back and repeat the process for each additional service later, but the curriculum's intent is to focus on one first to build the skill, get a working output, and create momentum, not to solve the whole agency at once.

  Hope that helps.
