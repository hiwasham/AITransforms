# AI Made Me the Bottleneck

> Announcement › Post 9 › by Ronnie › 2026-08-11

[Open on Circle](https://community.empowerlabs.ai/c/announcement/ai-made-me-the-bottleneck)

## Content

Most founders think becoming better at AI will make their company less dependent on them.

It can do the opposite.

The more effective you become with AI, the more your team can start depending on you for prompts, judgment, workflow design, and implementation.

The problem isn't becoming good at AI.

**It's becoming the AI translation layer for your entire company.**

In this video, Hayden explains how AI initially increased his personal leverage while making him an even bigger bottleneck—and what changed when the team stopped centering AI around him and started building AI capability across the organization.

### **In this video, you'll learn:**

🧠 Why becoming the best AI user in the company can actually increase founder dependency

⚙️ How to shift AI from being a founder's personal advantage into a capability that operators can build and use themselves

📈 How Andi built a workflow that eliminated manual reporting, saved roughly two hours per salesperson per week, and reduced reporting lag from 7 days to 1 day

📧 How Mick transformed a cold-calling process into a cold-email system that generated roughly 20x the output at about half the cost and with significantly fewer staff

🚀 Why AI-capable operators can create new forms of leverage and innovation that the founder may never have created alone

One of the biggest takeaways is that AI shouldn't just create **founder leverage—company leverage**. The real value comes when the people closest to the work can use AI to build workflows, improve decisions, and create leverage without constantly relying on the founder.

## **Ready to Build AI Capability Across Your Team?**

The goal isn't to become the smartest AI user in the building.

It's to create **two or three strong operators who can build leverage without you.**

Start identifying the workflows where your team currently depends on you for prompts, judgment, or implementation—and begin turning that knowledge into repeatable systems your operators can own.
