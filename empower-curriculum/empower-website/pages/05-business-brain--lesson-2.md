# The Business Framework

Before AI can help you run your business, it needs to understand how your business runs. In this session, we map your Customer Lifecycle and install the "Shared Language" of operations into your Master Prompt.

Exercise 2.1

## Know Your Customer Lifecycle

Imagine a business as a journey that follows a clear path. We need to map this journey before we can optimize it.

#### 1. Market to Lead

Connecting with potential customers. This is where marketing happens – letting people know we exist and what we offer.

#### 2. Lead to Sale

Understanding needs and showing how you help. If done well, the lead becomes a customer.

#### 3. Sale to Delivery

Providing what you promised. Shipping a product, implementing a service, or onboarding a new client.

#### 4. Delivery to Success

Ensuring they get value. Following up, providing support, and ensuring success.

### Beyond the Basics: Creating a Virtuous Cycle

In average businesses, the journey ends after delivery. Great businesses go beyond that and build a cycle that continuously builds on itself.

#### 5. Success to Lead

Happy customers become a source of new leads through:

-  Referrals to friends/colleagues

-  Testimonials & Trust

-  Repeat business & upsells

#### 6. Success to Market

Customer success feeds back into marketing:

-  Reviews boost reputation

-  Customer stories as content

-  Data refines messaging

#### Why This Cycle Matters

Constantly huntingAttracting customers

Tight marginsProfitable growth

Working harderWorking smarter

When your business processes form this virtuous cycle, each customer you serve makes it easier to find and serve the next one. Your business becomes more efficient, more profitable, and more enjoyable to run.

The most successful businesses aren't just good at each individual step – they're masterful at connecting these steps into a self-reinforcing cycle where success creates more success.

Exercise 2.2

## Explore the 3 Layers of Business Processes

Successful businesses don't just optimize the customer journey; they build a complete ecosystem where every function works together.

Layer 1 (Top)

### Strategic Planning Layer

Purpose: Setting direction and allocating resources. This top level guides everything below it, ensuring all business activities align with your long-term vision and goals.

#### Includes:

Strategic Planning, Vision/Mission Definition, Long-term Objective Setting

Layer 2 (Middle)

### The Customer Lifecycle

Purpose: Directly generating revenue and serving customers. This is the 6-stage cycle we just covered in Exercise 2.1.

Layer 3 (Bottom)

### Enabling Processes

Purpose: Supporting and strengthening the core business. These foundational processes may not directly generate revenue, but they're essential for operational excellence and long-term sustainability.

Order to Cash

Supply Chain & Asset Management

IT & Knowledge Management

Operational Scheduling

Financial Performance Mgmt

Sustainability & Env. Mgmt

Vendor/Supplier Management

Hire to Retire (HR)

Plan to Report

Partnerships Management

Risk/Crisis Management

Continuous Improvement

Quality Management

Legal & Compliance

### The Integrated System

What makes truly exceptional businesses stand out is how these layers work together.

#### Vertical Integration

Information flows both ways. Strategic plans guide operations; operational results inform strategy.

#### Horizontal Integration

Processes within each layer coordinate. Example: Resource Planning (HR) aligns with Financial Modeling (Finance).

#### Process Maturity

As businesses evolve, processes become more systematic, documented, measured, and optimized.

"By viewing your business as this integrated system... you can identify bottlenecks, opportunities, and ways to create more value with fewer resources."

Exercise 2.3

## Learn How RACI Connects People & Process

We need a practical way to manage who does what. Without clear roles, tasks fall through the cracks and decision-making stalls.

#### Why It Matters

-  No more "I thought you were doing that"

-  Eliminates duplicated efforts

-  Enforces accountability

R

Responsible

The person who actually does the work.

A

Accountable

The ONE person answerable for the outcome.

C

Consulted

People whose opinions are sought (2-way).

I

Informed

People kept up-to-date (1-way).

### Implementing RACI in Your Business

For each process in your business, you'll (eventually) need to identify:

Key activities or tasks

Roles involved

Who is R, A, C, and I

Don’t worry, this is a long-term goal!

However, for now, we do want to identify who is currently accountable for each process, and if the process currently doesn't exist in your business. This itself is valuable information—it highlights potential gaps in your business process architecture.

Accountable: N/ALegitimately not needed at this stage.

Accountable: FutureA gap that needs to be filled near-term.

Remember: The goal isn't to implement every possible process immediately, but to understand your current state and prioritize improvements that will deliver the most value.

# Style Preferences: - Be concise with your wording - ALWAYS ask me clarifying questions when I ask for something, and answer your own questions based on your knowledge. Then wait for my confirmation before proceeding. - When I ask for advice or for you to be a thought partner, I want you to be skeptical and conservative in your estimates. Not every idea is a good one. I want you to challenge me when it makes sense.
