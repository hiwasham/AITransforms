## The 4-Level Documentation Framework

Most documentation fails because it mixes strategy with tactics. This hierarchy separates them, allowing you to scale without chaos.

1

### Core Process (Strategic Overview)

Owner: Directors or Executives (Accountable for the entire function)

Audience: Leadership team, cross-functional stakeholders, process integrators

The 30,000ft view. Shows the complete flow of a critical business function (e.g., Sale to Delivery) including all stakeholders, KPIs, and handoffs.

Example: "Sale to Delivery - Product 1"

2

### Blueprint (Functional Breakdown)

Owner: Managers or Directors (Accountable for specific functional area)

Audience: Department managers, team leads, project coordinators

Breaks down Core Processes into manageable functional chunks. Focuses on coordination, quality gates, and resource requirements.

Example: "Payment Confirmation & Platform Setup"

3

### Guide (Step-by-Step Execution)

Owner: Managers or Senior Individual Contributors (Person who most often executes this activity)

Audience: Individual contributors, team members doing the actual work

Detailed instructions for specific activities. Includes tools, screenshots, and "how-to" steps for consistent execution.

Example: "Platform Account Creation Steps"

4

### Forms & Templates (Tools)

Owner: Individual Contributor (Person who most often uses this tool)

Audience: Anyone executing the specific task

The actual tools used to do the work. Email scripts, calculators, checklists, and document templates.

### How the Levels Work Together

#### Strategic Context Flows Down

Leadership uses Core Processes to understand business impact. Managers use Blueprints to coordinate their teams. Individual contributors use Guides to execute consistently.

#### Quality Flows Up

Templates ensure consistent output. Guides ensure consistent process. Blueprints ensure consistent coordination. Core Processes ensure consistent business results.

#### Efficiency Through Focus

Each level serves its specific audience without overwhelming them with unnecessary detail or leaving them without sufficient context.
