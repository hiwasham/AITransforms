The Business Framework

Before AI can help you run your business, it needs to understand how your business runs. In this session, we map your Customer Lifecycle and install the "Shared Language" of operations into your Master Prompt.

Exercise 2.1

Know Your Customer Lifecycle

Imagine a business as a journey that follows a clear path. We need to map this journey before we can optimize it.

1. Market to Lead

Connecting with potential customers. This is where marketing happens – letting people know we exist and what we offer.

2. Lead to Sale

Understanding needs and showing how you help. If done well, the lead becomes a customer.

3. Sale to Delivery

Providing what you promised. Shipping a product, implementing a service, or onboarding a new client.

4. Delivery to Success

Ensuring they get value. Following up, providing support, and ensuring success.

Beyond the Basics: Creating a Virtuous Cycle

In average businesses, the journey ends after delivery. Great businesses go beyond that and build a cycle that continuously builds on itself.

5. Success to Lead

Happy customers become a source of new leads through:

  • Referrals to friends/colleagues
  • Testimonials & Trust
  • Repeat business & upsells

6. Success to Market

Customer success feeds back into marketing:

  • Reviews boost reputation
  • Customer stories as content
  • Data refines messaging

Why This Cycle Matters

Constantly huntingAttracting customers
Tight marginsProfitable growth
Working harderWorking smarter

When your business processes form this virtuous cycle, each customer you serve makes it easier to find and serve the next one. Your business becomes more efficient, more profitable, and more enjoyable to run.

The most successful businesses aren't just good at each individual step – they're masterful at connecting these steps into a self-reinforcing cycle where success creates more success.

Exercise 2.2

Explore the 3 Layers of Business Processes

Successful businesses don't just optimize the customer journey; they build a complete ecosystem where every function works together.

Layer 1 (Top)

Strategic Planning Layer

Purpose: Setting direction and allocating resources. This top level guides everything below it, ensuring all business activities align with your long-term vision and goals.

Includes:

Strategic Planning, Vision/Mission Definition, Long-term Objective Setting

Layer 2 (Middle)

The Customer Lifecycle

Purpose: Directly generating revenue and serving customers. This is the 6-stage cycle we just covered in Exercise 2.1.

Layer 3 (Bottom)

Enabling Processes

Purpose: Supporting and strengthening the core business. These foundational processes may not directly generate revenue, but they're essential for operational excellence and long-term sustainability.

Order to Cash
Supply Chain & Asset Management
IT & Knowledge Management
Operational Scheduling
Financial Performance Mgmt
Sustainability & Env. Mgmt
Vendor/Supplier Management
Hire to Retire (HR)
Plan to Report
Partnerships Management
Risk/Crisis Management
Continuous Improvement
Quality Management
Legal & Compliance

The Integrated System

What makes truly exceptional businesses stand out is how these layers work together.

Vertical Integration

Information flows both ways. Strategic plans guide operations; operational results inform strategy.

Horizontal Integration

Processes within each layer coordinate. Example: Resource Planning (HR) aligns with Financial Modeling (Finance).

Process Maturity

As businesses evolve, processes become more systematic, documented, measured, and optimized.

"By viewing your business as this integrated system... you can identify bottlenecks, opportunities, and ways to create more value with fewer resources."
Exercise 2.3

Learn How RACI Connects People & Process

We need a practical way to manage who does what. Without clear roles, tasks fall through the cracks and decision-making stalls.

Why It Matters

  • No more "I thought you were doing that"
  • Eliminates duplicated efforts
  • Enforces accountability
R
Responsible

The person who actually does the work.

A
Accountable

The ONE person answerable for the outcome.

C
Consulted

People whose opinions are sought (2-way).

I
Informed

People kept up-to-date (1-way).

Implementing RACI in Your Business

For each process in your business, you'll (eventually) need to identify:

Key activities or tasks
Roles involved
Who is R, A, C, and I

Don’t worry, this is a long-term goal!

However, for now, we do want to identify who is currently accountable for each process, and if the process currently doesn't exist in your business. This itself is valuable information—it highlights potential gaps in your business process architecture.

Accountable: N/ALegitimately not needed at this stage.
Accountable: FutureA gap that needs to be filled near-term.

Remember: The goal isn't to implement every possible process immediately, but to understand your current state and prioritize improvements that will deliver the most value.

Business_Framework.txt
# 📦Business Process Framework

This document outlines the strategic and operational process architecture of the business, organized into three tiers: **Strategic Planning**, **Customer Lifecycle**, and **Enabling Processes**. It shows who in the organization is Responsible, Accountable and Consulted in each process, and if there is no one there, or it says X or Future, it means we plan on updating it in the future.

## 🧠 STRATEGIC PLANNING 

Strategic Planning defines the company's direction, resource allocation, and long-term positioning.

Environmental Scanning: Monitoring external factors including market trends, competitive landscape, regulatory changes, and emerging technologies to identify opportunities and threats
Vision, Mission & Values Definition: Establishing and refining the organization's long-term vision, core mission, and fundamental values that guide decision-making and culture
Customer & Market Strategy: Defining target customer segments, market positioning, and competitive differentiation strategies to drive sustainable growth
Brand Strategy: Developing and maintaining brand identity, messaging, and positioning to create consistent market presence and customer perception
Strategy Formulation: Translating vision and market analysis into specific strategic initiatives, priorities, and resource allocation decisions
Innovation Strategy: Planning and prioritizing new product/service development, technology adoption, and process improvements to maintain competitive advantage
New Product/Service Strategy: Systematic approach to identifying, developing, and launching new offerings that align with market needs and business objectives
Goal Setting & Strategic Initiative Planning: Converting strategic direction into specific, measurable objectives and coordinated action plans with clear ownership and timelines
Strategy Communication & Alignment: Ensuring strategic priorities and objectives are clearly communicated and understood across all levels of the organization

## 🔁 CORE VALUE CHAIN: The Customer Lifecycle

These represent the **customer lifecycle** from initial awareness to advocacy and re-engagement.

### 🎯 1. MARKET TO LEAD  
**Goal:** Generate interest and turn strangers into MQLs  
**Buyer Stage:** Awareness → Interest  

✅ Market Research & ICP Definition
✅ Positioning, Messaging & Channel Strategy
✅ Content Development & Campaigns
✅ Paid / Owned / Earned Media
✅ SEO & CRO
✅ Brand Monitoring & Sentiment Tracking
✅ Marketing Automation & Lead Capture
✅ Attribution & ROI Analytics
**🔄 Output →** Marketing Qualified Leads (MQLs)

### 🤝 2. LEAD TO SALE 
**Goal:** Convert MQLs into paying customers  
**Buyer Stage:** Interest → Evaluation → Purchase  

✅ Lead Scoring & Qualification
✅ Owned Media Content Marketing
✅ Owned Media Direct Outreach
✅ SDR / Setter Process
✅ Sales Enablement & Collateral
✅ Discovery & Demo Calls
✅ Objection Handling & Follow-up
✅ Quoting & Proposal Management
✅ CRM Pipeline Reviews & Forecasting
✅ Sales Team Coaching
**🔄 Output →** Signed Contract / Closed-Won Deal

### 🛫 3. SALE TO DELIVERY 
**Goal:** Seamlessly onboard clients and prepare for delivery  
**Buyer Stage:** Purchase → Onboarding  

✅ Contract Finalization & Internal Handoff
✅ Welcome Sequences / Client Onboarding
✅ Scheduling & Delivery Planning
✅ Success Metric Definition
✅ Asset / Info Collection
✅ Journey Mapping & Team Assignment
**🔄 Output →** Delivery Kickoff + Fully Onboarded Client

### 🧩 4. DELIVERY TO SUCCESS  
**Goal:** Deliver value and build long-term satisfaction  
**Buyer Stage:** Onboarding → Value Realization  

✅ Fulfillment / Service Execution
✅ KPI Tracking & Reporting
✅ QA / Approvals
✅ Customer Success Management
✅ Feedback Collection (NPS, CSAT)
✅ Issue Resolution
✅ Retention Strategies
**🔄 Output →** Measured Success + Retained Client

### 🔁 5. SUCCESS TO LEAD   
**Goal:** Convert satisfied customers into growth engines  
**Buyer Stage:** Value Realization → Advocacy  

✅ Case Study / Testimonial Gathering
✅ Review & Referral Program
✅ Cross-Sell / Upsell Opportunities
✅ Customer Community Engagement
✅ Ambassador & Affiliate Strategy
✅ Co-Marketing & Advocate Activation
**🔄 Output →** New Referrals + Expansion Revenue

### 🔄 6. SUCCESS TO MARKET  
**Goal:** Feed advocacy back into the top of the funnel  

Past clients refer others → re-enter **Lead to Sale**
Upsold clients re-enter **Sale to Delivery**
Reviewers generate organic MQLs → re-enter **Market to Lead**
## 🛠 ENABLING PROCESSES

These processes provide **infrastructure, insight, and capacity** to support the Core and Strategic functions.

Order to Cash: Managing the financial flow from customer orders to revenue collection
Supply Chain & Asset Management: Sourcing, storing, and maintaining the physical resources needed to deliver value
Information Technology & Knowledge Management: IT operations, security, system maintenance, and organizing data and insights to support decision-making across the business
Operational Scheduling & Deployment: Scheduling and deploying operational resources including workforce, equipment, machinery, vehicles, and tools to ensure efficient service delivery and project execution
Financial Performance Management: Tracking, analyzing, and optimizing financial outcomes
Sustainability & Environmental Management: Minimizing negative impacts and creating positive change
Vendor/Supplier Management: Managing vendor relationships and expenditures
Hire to Retire: Building and supporting your team through recruitment, development, and offboarding
Plan to Report: Setting goals and measuring progress through KPIs and reporting
Partnerships Management: Developing and maintaining strategic relationships with other organizations
Risk/Crisis Management: Identifying, mitigating, and responding to potential threats
Continuous Improvement: Systematically enhancing processes across all areas of the business
Quality Management: Establishing standards, implementing control systems, and continuously monitoring to ensure consistent delivery of products/services that meet or exceed customer expectations across all business functions
Legal & Compliance: Managing regulatory requirements, contract administration, intellectual property protection, and ensuring adherence to applicable laws and industry standards across all business operations

# Style Preferences:

- Be concise with your wording - ALWAYS ask me clarifying questions when I ask for something, and answer your own questions based on your knowledge. Then wait for my confirmation before proceeding.
- When I ask for advice or for you to be a thought partner, I want you to be skeptical and conservative in your estimates. Not every idea is a good one. I want you to challenge me when it makes sense.

# Strategic Approaches:

- Whenever I ask for a 1-3-1, I am asking you to help with a Decision-making Framework in which you determine the problem, the outcome and provide, 3 potential paths and 1 recommended course of action. Exercise 2.5: Add Your Resources

One last step to complete your Master Prompt.

Purpose

To add information about your available resources to your Master Prompt so Claude can take these constraints into consideration.

Instructions

  • Copy and paste the prompt into a NEW chat within your Master Prompt project

Resources.txt
Help me create a list of my organization's resources. Ask me questions, one by one, to identify our:

- Capital: Cash, credit, investment funds
- Physical: Equipment, facilities, inventory
- Intellectual: Patents, copyrights, trade secrets, customer lists
- Brand: Reputation, trust, customer relationships
- People: Employees with their skills, knowledge, relationships (you already know some of the people in my organization)

Then compile my responses into a separate artifact.
