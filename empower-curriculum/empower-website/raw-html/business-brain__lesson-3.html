# Sale to Delivery Assessment

## Introduction
This assessment focuses on the critical transition period from when a sale is closed to when actual delivery/execution begins. We're evaluating how effectively you move customers from 'yes' to 'ready for work to begin' - including contract handling, onboarding, information gathering, expectation setting, and planning.

For the purpose of this assessment, the 'Sale to Delivery' process ends when the actual delivery work begins. The subsequent 'Delivery to Success' process focuses on the execution of work and ensuring customer satisfaction.

Note: Adapt these concepts to my business model - whether I deliver products, services, experiences, or combinations thereof. Focus on my primary customer interaction and value delivery method.

- **Name of Product/Service**: [Input, text]
- **Brief Description of Product/Service**: [Input, text]

### Scoring Framework
Each question uses a scale of 7-5-3-1-0, where:
- **7 points = Excellent systems and processes in place**
- **5 points = Good systems that need minor improvements**
- **3 points = Basic systems exist but need significant improvement**
- **1 point = Minimal or ad-hoc approach**
- **0 points = No systems exist or unknown**

For each question, you'll also evaluate owner involvement using the RACI framework:
- **Not at all / Informed (one-way communication only) = 5 points**
- **Consulted (provides input but isn't directly involved) = 3 points**
- **Accountable (has final approval/oversight but doesn't do the work) = 1 point**
- **Responsible (actively does the work) = 0 points**

Higher scores on owner involvement indicate better delegation and less owner-dependent processes.

Always ask one question at a time (consisting of the process evaluation and the owner's involvement) and await my answer before moving on to the next.

The 4-Level Documentation Framework

Most documentation fails because it mixes strategy with tactics. This hierarchy separates them, allowing you to scale without chaos.

1

Core Process (Strategic Overview)

Owner: Directors or Executives (Accountable for the entire function)
Audience: Leadership team, cross-functional stakeholders, process integrators

The 30,000ft view. Shows the complete flow of a critical business function (e.g., Sale to Delivery) including all stakeholders, KPIs, and handoffs.

Example: "Sale to Delivery - Product 1"
2

Blueprint (Functional Breakdown)

Owner: Managers or Directors (Accountable for specific functional area)
Audience: Department managers, team leads, project coordinators

Breaks down Core Processes into manageable functional chunks. Focuses on coordination, quality gates, and resource requirements.

Example: "Payment Confirmation & Platform Setup"
3

Guide (Step-by-Step Execution)

Owner: Managers or Senior Individual Contributors (Person who most often executes this activity)
Audience: Individual contributors, team members doing the actual work

Detailed instructions for specific activities. Includes tools, screenshots, and "how-to" steps for consistent execution.

Example: "Platform Account Creation Steps"
4

Forms & Templates (Tools)

Owner: Individual Contributor (Person who most often uses this tool)
Audience: Anyone executing the specific task

The actual tools used to do the work. Email scripts, calculators, checklists, and document templates.

Example: "Welcome Email Template"

How the Levels Work Together

Strategic Context Flows Down

Leadership uses Core Processes to understand business impact. Managers use Blueprints to coordinate their teams. Individual contributors use Guides to execute consistently.

Quality Flows Up

Templates ensure consistent output. Guides ensure consistent process. Blueprints ensure consistent coordination. Core Processes ensure consistent business results.

Efficiency Through Focus

Each level serves its specific audience without overwhelming them with unnecessary detail or leaving them without sufficient context.

Implementation in Your Business

Step #1

Start with What Matters Most

Begin by identifying your most critical customer-facing processes.

  • How leads become customers (Lead to Sale)
  • How customers get onboarded (Sale to Delivery)
  • How you deliver your core service (Delivery to Success)
Step #2

Build from Level 1 Down

  • Map the Core ProcessUnderstand the complete flow and all stakeholders
  • Identify Key BlueprintsBreak down into manageable functional areas
  • Create Essential GuidesDocument the most critical execution steps
  • Build Supporting TemplatesCreate tools that ensure quality and efficiency
Step #3

Use AI to Accelerate Development

With your Master Prompt and business context, AI can help you:

  • Generate complete process flows from high-level descriptions
  • Create detailed guides from blueprint overviews
  • Build templates and forms based on guide requirements
  • Identify gaps and improvement opportunities across all levels

Why This System Creates Competitive Advantage

For Remote Teams

Everyone has both context (why this matters) and clarity (exactly how to do it), eliminating the confusion that kills remote productivity.

For Scaling Operations

You can train new team members faster and maintain quality as you grow, without requiring constant management oversight.

For Customer Experience

Consistent processes across all levels ensure every customer receives the same high-quality experience regardless of who serves them.

For Strategic Agility

When you need to change how something works, you can see the full impact across all levels and make coordinated updates rather than hoping nothing breaks.
