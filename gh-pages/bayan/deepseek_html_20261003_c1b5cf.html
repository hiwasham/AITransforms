02 Baseline arithmetic

Computed from the saved paste files — not from the rounded prose claims. Four things the arithmetic actually shows.

01 The payer count is fewer than 13 — not "about 13"
if <1% uses 1,300 as denominator:
0.01 × 1,300 = 13
→ payers = < 13

Not a verified payer count — the rate window and numerator are missing. The value is a ceiling, not a measurement.

02 $277 ÷ 1,300 is not ARPU
$277 ÷ 1,300 = $0.2130769…
≈ $0.2131 per registered user

Across an unknown "to date" window. This is not ARPU, ARPPU, MRR, LTV, or revenue per eligible user. It is a raw ratio of two numbers with different meanings.

03 "+500 visits → +$10–50/mo" cannot be checked
missing: per-payer price · conversion window
missing: repeat / retention behavior
missing: acquisition source

It illustrates missing inputs — not an evidence-based result.

04 "1% → 5% = thousands/mo" depends on invalid assumptions
@ hypothetical $16/mo, all 1,300 eligible:
1% → 13 payers → $208 gross run-rate
5% → 65 payers → $1,040 gross run-rate
gap = $832 gross monthly run-rate

Before fees, refunds, churn, eligibility exclusions. A hypothetical on an invalid-until-verified denominator — not a Bayan projection.

03 Cohort model · the real denominator

The conversion denominator is not 1,300. Deduplicate the account export, then assign every account to exactly one mutually exclusive eligibility group at a fixed snapshot date.

"Wrong segment" is a declared-intent / offer-fit attribute to check — not a reason to silently drop a user.

The supplied material proposes a cumulative conversion scenario of 3% / 5% / 7% over three months on an addressable base, at a blended ~$16. For arithmetic only, A = 400 is used as a placeholder — replace it with the Week-1 addressable count before any number here is used.

Read two things honestly: the 12 / 20 / 28 are cumulative — only 12 / 8 / 8 are new; and $192 / $320 / $448 are run-rate levels, not new money added each month. Month 1's $192 does not exceed the $277 lifetime-to-date — and the two are not comparable in any case.
if one-time $16 = a one-time exam pass These are cumulative gross receipts — not MRR. Do not call them MRR.
Month 1 Month 2 Month 3
Cumulative gross receipts $192 $320 $448
Added receipts this month $192 $128 $128
MRR n/a n/a n/a
There is no run-rate; a one-time pass does not recur. Report as receipts, not as MRR. The word "MRR" is not allowed anywhere near these numbers.
⚑
Never compare monthly to lifetime

Never compare a monthly MRR run-rate to the $277 lifetime-to-date. First establish the window the $277 covers; then compare it only to cumulative receipts over the same window. Monthly run-rate vs lifetime total is a category error, not a result.

◆
If the mix includes both — report three quantities, never one blended number

MRR = active recurring subscriptions × net monthly price (after churn). One-time receipts = exam-pass units × net pass price, in the month collected. Net contribution = receipts − (payment fees + variable service / content cost) per unit.

The 3 / 5 / 7% figures are proposed scenarios from the supplied material, not committed targets and not a forecast. They become targets only after Week-1 instrumentation gives Bayan's own baseline on the fixed addressable denominator.

"Good" is 4–5%; best-in-class 6–8% — AI-native products skew toward that top band. Bayan at <1% sits below even the EdTech average, so the headroom is real. A re-engaged, nurtured base with a working paywall and a mid-tier SKU can plausibly reach 3–5% of the addressable cohort within 90 days.

A benchmark explains why the headroom exists — it does not validate 3/5/7% for Bayan over 90 days. Only Bayan's own instrumented baseline can do that.

⚠
Data gate · turn scenario → target

Four things must be known before any rate here is more than illustrative: (1) fixed A; (2) baseline numerator and denominator with closed date windows; (3) verified net price; (4) recurring-vs-one-time product mix.

06 CAC and unit economics

The distinction that matters: media CAC and fully-loaded CAC are different numbers, and the existing-base conversion is not $0 CAC even when media spend is $0.

$0 paid media is not $0 CAC
$0 media
Fully-loaded program cost is real — Nasim's fee + any engineering + tooling + payment fees. Report media CAC and fully-loaded CAC separately. It remains the cheapest MRR available because it converts users already in the door.
New paid acquisition · Months 2–3
Held at $0 until the funnel proves organic conversion
$10–150 / signup
EdTech CAC runs ~$10–150 per signup / trial and ~$50–300 per paying customer (verified 2026-10-01). Defensible only once LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1 with payback inside 12 months is demonstrable — which needs retention data Bayan does not yet have.
⚑
Guardrail, not a measured ratio

LTV:CAC cannot be claimed before retention and margin data exist. State it as a guardrail — not as a Bayan-measured ratio. It gates paid acquisition; it does not describe current performance.
