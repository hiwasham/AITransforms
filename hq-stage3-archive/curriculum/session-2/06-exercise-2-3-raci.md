# Exercise 2.3: Learn How RACI Connects People and Process

Now that we understand the full architecture of business processes, we need a practical way to manage who does what within each process. That's where RACI comes in.

---

## What is RACI?

RACI is a responsibility assignment framework that brings clarity to process roles. The acronym stands for:

- **R - Responsible**: The person who actually does the work to achieve the task
- **A - Accountable**: The person who is ultimately answerable for the completion and quality of the deliverable (there should be only ONE accountable person for each task)
- **C - Consulted**: People whose opinions are sought during the process (two-way communication)
- **I - Informed**: People who are kept up-to-date on progress (one-way communication)

---

## Why RACI Matters

Without clear responsibility assignment:

- Tasks fall through the cracks ("I thought someone else was handling that")
- Multiple people duplicate efforts on the same task
- Decision-making becomes slow or stalled
- Accountability becomes impossible to enforce

RACI provides a simple but powerful framework to eliminate these issues. It also gives us a great framework to work with when we look at how AI can take over certain elements of process.

---

## Implementing RACI in Your Business

For each process in your business, you'll (eventually) need to identify:

1. The key activities or tasks within that process
2. The roles involved in that process
3. Who is R, A, C, and I for each task

Don't worry, this is a long-term goal!

However, for now, we do want to identify who is currently _accountable_ for each process, and if the process currently doesn't exist in your business. This itself is valuable information - it highlights potential gaps in your business process architecture.

If a process does not currently exist in your organization, you can mark it as "Accountable: N/A" if it legitimately isn't needed in your business model at this stage. If it doesn't exist, but it represents a gap that needs to be filled in the near-term, you can mark it as "Accountable: Future."

Remember: The goal isn't to implement every possible process immediately, but to understand your current state and prioritize improvements that will deliver the most value.

> **Instructor note**: We'll be adopting RACI as an accountability framework, but will focus on the Responsible, Accountable, and Consulted roles, ignoring the Informed category since any number of people can be informed about a process without it impacting how that process functions.
