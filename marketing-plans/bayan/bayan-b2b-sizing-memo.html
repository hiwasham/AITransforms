01 Exec verdict (sized)

⚠
Seat revenue alone does not pay for a dedicated track

Full-GCC TAM ≈ $7.6M–$12.5M/yr at 100% penetration (fantasy ceiling), serviceable SAM ≈ $1.1M–$3.0M/yr, and realistically obtainable by a 10 hr/week team in 12–24 months (SOM) ≈ $5k–$86k/yr ARR — with ~$0 year-1 cash because Bayan currently gives free 1-year institutional trials. A single landed pilot nets roughly $1,200–$7,200/yr gross (20–120 seats × ~$60 blended), minus the CEO's 20% commission.

So on seat revenue alone, B2B does not pay for a dedicated near-term cash track. It is defensible as priority #1 only if valued as a credibility / distribution / moat play — anchor logos, bulk free-seat user acquisition that feeds the B2C funnel, co-marketing with named institutions, and a springboard to negotiated site-license / CME contracts priced far above the per-seat list (the list price is a floor, not a ceiling).

The single number that flips the verdict from "strategic, small" to "material revenue" is average negotiated contract value per institution — if that is a flat site/CME deal in the tens of thousands rather than 40 seats × $60, the prize is 10–50× larger. That number is unknown today and is the thing to go get.

Key competitive finding

Regional incumbents are B2C-only — none publishes an institutional/seat price. The institutional lane is largely uncontested regionally, but that may be because Gulf institutions don't currently buy seat licenses, not because no one sells to them. The only credible institutional sellers (AMBOSS, Lecturio, Osmosis) are global, not Gulf-exam-specific, priced by negotiation. Bayan's wedge (internal positioning): Oman-built, Gulf-exam-specific, clinician-reviewed vs foreign tools that "don't fit Gulf exams."

◆
Where the economics could work

(a) A pilot's free seats are a bulk B2C acquisition channel — convert even 10–20% of trial users to $69–$199/yr individual subs and the pilot is worth 3–10× the seat fee; (b) negotiated flat site/CME contracts above list; (c) anchor logos that lower B2C CAC. None captured in the seat-price model.

07 Recommendation · the prize + first moves

How big is the prize

B2B is priority #1 — the justification must be strategic, not seat cash

Direct seat revenue is modest — SAM ~$1–3M/yr, obtainable SOM <$90k/yr ARR for a 10 hr/week team, ~$0 year-1 cash. If the CEO ranks B2B above B2C, the justification is not near-term seat cash; it must be strategic: anchor logos + clinician/institutional relationships (match the CEO's and Nasim's network and selling style), bulk free-seat user acquisition feeding the B2C funnel, and a path to negotiated site-license/CME contracts the per-seat list floors but does not cap. Framed that way, B2B-first is coherent — provided success is measured in logos, converted trial-users, and one lighthouse contract, not seat-fee revenue.

First 1–2 moves (fits 10 hr/week, min-20-seat 1-yr pilots):

#1
Convert the warm book first — do not prospect cold. Pull the list of institutions already on free 1-yr trials (unknown #6) + the Oman/MedResearch Academy relationships, and run a renewal/expansion conversation on the two or three warmest. Only motion that produces revenue (or a real contract-value data point — unknown #1) inside 12 months with near-zero selling hours.
One signed lighthouse contract with a named Gulf institution.
#2
Instrument trial-seat → B2C conversion. Before scaling pilots, track free institutional seats so you can prove the real payoff (B2C conversions + usage), per the funnel-instrumentation gate in revenue-integration-strategy.md. Turns the free-trial model from a cost into a measurable acquisition channel and produces the contract-value/conversion numbers that currently make the prize unknowable.
Tracked funnel + conversion data.
◆
The one piece of data that changes the answer

Average negotiated contract value per institution (unknown #1). If lighthouse deals land as flat site/CME licenses in the tens of thousands rather than ~$2k seat bundles, the prize is 10–50× the seat model and B2B-first is justified on revenue alone — go get that number from move #1.
