01 · The one-minute answerYou were right — and there is a fact under it that changes the plan.

Read this box and you know the whole strategy; everything after it is evidence and schedule.

Paid-access enforcement began 1 May 2026. Every account created before that keeps full free access, permanently — plus 13 humanitarian free-access countries. So a large share of the 1,300 cannot pay: they already have everything. The real, convertible base is far smaller, and it has never been measured. So Week 1 is not copywriting — it is segmentation + funnel instrumentation first, then the conversion levers aimed only at users who can actually buy. On the levers themselves: activation (first value in session one), lifecycle nurture (trial→paid sequence), and a mid-tier price rung. Zero ad spend throughout.

›Lead→Sale — assessment findings + fixesopen for detail

What the assessment found (ranked by revenue blocked):

  • No measurement — the funnel emits no events; "why don't they pay" is currently unanswerable with data.
  • Grandfathering + free-country structure — a large share of the 1,300 already has full free access (your decision; we pull numbers and propose only).
  • No trial→paid trigger — no paywall moment, no "trial ends in 3 days," no upgrade nudge.
  • No nurture — users sign up, hit the 5-question/day wall, go quiet. No onboarding, no re-engagement.
  • Price-ladder gap — free jumps straight to $9.99–29/mo; no single-exam pass for the candidate who needs one exam.
  • Facts drift — the question count shows 8 different values across live surfaces, quietly undercutting every landing page.

What the 30 days do about it: instrumentation + segmentation (W1) → activation + trial→paid SOP + paywall (W2) → winback drip + mid-tier SKU (W3) → measured results and a go/no-go (W4). Every fix lands with a number attached, and a standing experiment backlog (headline tests, pricing-page tests, paywall copy tests, form friction) so improvement continues after the sprint instead of ending with it.

›Sale→Delivery — assessment findings + fixesopen for detail

What the assessment found:

  • The payment path itself is unverified — no test purchase has ever been recorded end-to-end.
  • First value is unmeasured and slow — mobile load is 5.0 seconds (loses the candidate before the first question); no confirmation a new payer reaches their first answered question in session one.
  • Three screens decide most of the revenue — first-login diagnostic, the "your gap" screen, and the paywall. These are currently the weakest screens in the flow.

What the 30 days do about it: a live checkout test purchase (W1), a first-value audit with a documented path and blocker list (W2), and a redesign of the three revenue screens — the mockups you have already seen and liked are the direction. If you approve the revenue-flow redesign, we recommend doing the whole product UI as one consistent system, not a patched section, so the app doesn't read as two different products.

Build or hand off — your choice. We deliver the designs and the solutions; we can implement them ourselves, or hand them to your existing team ready-to-build. The same established system we used to produce all the work in §04 keeps the effort and calendar short either way — it is a repeat of a working machine, not a from-scratch build.
