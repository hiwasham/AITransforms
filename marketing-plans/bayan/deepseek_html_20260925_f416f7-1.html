Define CRM Data Standards & Stage Requirements

Purpose: To document the mandatory fields, stage definitions, and exit criteria for every lead and deal record in the CRM. This standard is the target that all enrichment, audit, and automation processes enforce — and the single source of truth for how the sales pipeline actually works.

Owner (Responsible): amin jabari
Accountable: amin jabari
Review Frequency: Quarterly (or when pipeline stages or offer structure change)

1. Prerequisites

  • Lead to Sale process documented (Level 1 + Level 2 already exist).
  • CRM access with admin permissions.
  • Sample of 20–30 real historical deals that moved successfully through the pipeline.
  • Sample of 5–10 deals that stalled or were lost, to identify where the standard should have flagged them.
  • Access to salespeople (or transcripts) to extract the current "tribal knowledge" of how the pipeline actually works.

2. Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Capture the Current "As-Is" Reality
    • Don't try to fix the mess before documenting it. Instead, extract it.
    • Record a walkthrough with each salesperson (or yourself) of how deals actually move through the CRM today.
    • Feed the transcript into AI with your Master Prompt context and ask it to structure the messy input into a draft pipeline.
    • The AI acts as the "organized brain" — you provide the raw reality, it produces structure.
  2. Define the Pipeline Stages
    • Write out every stage a lead passes through from first contact to closed-won. Use 5–7 stages; more than that and people stop updating.
    • Standard stages for a Lead to Sale pipeline (adapt to your business):
      • New Lead — just entered CRM, not yet contacted.
      • Qualified — passed scoring, ready for discovery.
      • Discovery Scheduled — call booked.
      • Proposal Sent — proposal delivered, awaiting decision.
      • Negotiation — active back-and-forth on pricing or scope.
      • Closed Won — signed, moving to delivery.
      • Closed Lost — declined or gone dark.
    • Write a one-sentence definition for each stage in plain language.
  3. Define Mandatory Fields per Stage
    • For each stage, list the fields that must be populated before a record is considered "complete."
    • Example for Qualified stage:
      • Lead source
      • Contact email and phone
      • Company / institution name
      • Stated goal
      • Lead score
      • Qualification criteria met (Y/N)
    • Example for Proposal Sent stage:
      • All Qualified fields plus:
      • Proposal sent date
      • Proposal value
      • Decision-maker identified (Y/N)
      • Expected decision date
    • Aim for 4–8 mandatory fields per stage. Anything more and compliance drops.
  4. Define Exit Criteria (Advancement Rules)
    • For each stage, define what must be TRUE for a record to move forward to the next stage.
    • Example exit criteria for Discovery Scheduled → Proposal Sent:
      • Discovery call completed.
      • Discovery notes logged in CRM.
      • Decision-maker confirmed.
      • Proposal value agreed with prospect (or estimated).
    • Exit criteria prevent "stage creep" — deals sitting in the wrong stage because no one defined when they should move.
  5. Define AI Write Permissions per Field
    • For each field, decide whether AI can update it autonomously or needs human confirmation.
    • AI can write: Call notes, enrichment data (emails, phones, LinkedIn), next-step reminders, engagement signals.
    • AI can suggest (human approves): Stage change, lead score update.
    • Human-only: Pricing, contract terms, Closed Won status, disqualification reason.
    • This protects against AI overreach on high-stakes decisions.
  6. Document the Standard
    • Package the standard as a structured document (Markdown or HTML) with four sections:
      • Pipeline stages and definitions
      • Mandatory fields per stage
      • Exit criteria per stage
      • AI write permissions per field
    • Save to ~/proposal-system/crm-standard/ or your CRM automation folder.
    • Also load it into the Master Prompt so any AI agent that touches CRM has the standard in context.
  7. Configure the CRM to Enforce the Standard
    • In the CRM (Vtiger / HubSpot / Salesforce), update the pipeline stages to match the documented stages.
    • Mark mandatory fields as required at each stage transition (most CRMs support this).
    • Remove or archive non-standard custom fields that fragment data.
    • Add validation rules where possible so a record can't advance without meeting exit criteria.
  8. Communicate the Standard
    • Share the standard with anyone using the CRM.
    • Walk through 2–3 real deals as examples: this is what a compliant "Qualified" record looks like, this is what's missing, here's the fix.
    • Make it clear that the standard is not optional — the hygiene audit and Management-by-Exception alerts will enforce it.
  9. Validate with a Test Batch
    • Take 10 recent records and score them against the new standard.
    • If more than 50% fail, the standard is too strict or the fields are wrong — revise before rolling out.
    • If all pass, the standard may not be catching anything — sharpen exit criteria.
    • Target: 70–90% pass on first rollout.
  10. Review & Maintain (Quarterly)
    • Review the standard quarterly against real sales behavior.
    • Look for fields that are always empty (remove them) or rules that always get overridden (rethink them).
    • Update the standard and re-configure the CRM. Version the document.
    • Announce changes to the team before enforcing.

3. Quality Checkpoints

  • Every pipeline stage has a plain-language definition that a new hire could apply correctly.
  • Every mandatory field has a clear reason for being mandatory — not just "nice to have."
  • Exit criteria are observable and testable (not subjective).
  • AI write permissions are documented field-by-field, not at a blanket level.
  • The standard passes the test batch at 70–90% on first rollout.
  • The standard fits within the CRM's native capabilities — no need for custom code to enforce.

4. Common Issues & Escalations

IssueAction
Too many mandatory fields → team stops entering anythingCut to the 4–5 most important per stage. Reintroduce after adoption stabilizes.
Stage definitions are ambiguousRewrite in plain language. Test with a new team member: can they place 5 sample deals correctly?
Salespeople use their own stagesArchive old stages in the CRM. Make the new ones the only option in the dropdown.
Exit criteria too strict → deals stall at the boundaryLoosen to "required" rather than "nice" criteria. Add the strict version as a "should have" flag instead.
AI overwrites human-edited fieldsUpdate write permissions: mark human-owned fields as read-only for AI.
Standard out of sync with the actual sales processQuarterly review, not annual. Check in on it every 90 days.
Nobody follows the standard after rolloutEnforce via hygiene audit and alerts. Standards without enforcement decay in 2 weeks.
