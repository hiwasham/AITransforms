Revenue Integration Strategy

How four archived models become one Bayan revenue machine.

Bayan already owns the one layer every other startup lacks — a trustworthy, clinician-gated content moat — and is missing the three layers the other archives have each perfected: demand, packaging, and engine.

The thesis

Most startups have a funnel and no moat. Bayan is the reverse.

The play is to bolt proven funnel, packaging, and agent machinery onto a moat that already exists — not to build a moat from scratch.

07 Guardrails

Non-negotiable for a medical brand. Every aggressive tactic runs inside these.

  1. No pass / outcome guarantee ever — cap at access- or effort-based refunds.
  2. No fake scarcity / countdown timers around a licensing career.
  3. Never bare "AI-powered" — always "AI assists with drafting; humans own what reaches learners."
  4. AI coach never invents clinical facts — cites reviewed content only.
  5. Localize prices far below Empower's US anchors ($9,998 cohort / $999 mo). Copy the ladder shape, not the numbers.
  6. Don't over-paywall — let candidates feel the clinical rigor before the wall.
  7. Keep MedResearch Academy a separate brand. Don't cite SQUH / OMSB / SQU as the founder's employer. Segment by exam path, not "speaks to everyone."

08 What to cut if time runs short

Cut order · keep Phase 0 + Phase 1 only

The three must-dos, in order.

Everything else is high-effort and none of it pays off until Phase 0 measurement exists anyway.

  1. Instrument the funnel + lock the question number. You can't grow what you can't see.
  2. Moat-as-hero + education drip. Uses the 472 articles already owned. Zero net-new content.
  3. Free diagnostic lead magnet. One build, three engines, top of funnel forever.
Cut to later: cohort, full AI agent, and B2B. Higher effort — and none pays off until Phase 0 measurement exists anyway.
