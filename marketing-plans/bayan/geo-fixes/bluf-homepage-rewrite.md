# GEO fix #5 — BLUF (answer-first) structure

**Problem (from audit):** fewer than 25% of pages lead with the answer. AI answer
engines (and skim-reading humans) extract the first 1–2 sentences under a
heading. If those sentences are throat-clearing ("Welcome to…", "In today's
competitive…"), there is nothing quotable, and Bayan gets skipped even when the
page is relevant.

**Rule:** every page and every major section starts with one or two sentences
that directly answer the implied question. Detail, story, and calls-to-action
come *after* the answer.

---

## Homepage — before → after

**Before (typical hero pattern, not answer-first):**
> Welcome to Bayan. Your journey to medical excellence starts here. Discover a
> world of learning designed for the modern clinician.

**After (BLUF):**
> **Bayan is a clinician-built platform for Gulf licensing, board, and nursing
> exam preparation.** Practicing physicians and nurse educators write and review
> every question, mapped to your exam's blueprint — 5,600+ questions, OSCE
> stations, virtual patients, 190+ drug monographs, and courses in one place.

## Section pattern — apply to each pathway block

Lead each pathway section with a direct sentence naming the exam and what's
covered, then expand:

- **Gulf licensing / Prometric:** "Bayan prepares candidates for DHA, MOH,
  SCFHS/SMLE, QCHP, OMSB, and NHRA licensing exams, with question banks mapped
  to each authority's blueprint." → then details.
- **Nursing:** "Bayan covers OEN, SNLE, DHA nursing, and NCLEX-RN with
  clinician-reviewed questions and rationales." → then details.
- **Postgraduate:** "Bayan gives residents adaptive, blueprint-aligned board
  preparation with clinical cases and OSCE practice." → then details.
- **Medical students:** "Bayan offers preclinical and clinical question banks
  and structured courses for medical students." → then details.

## Checklist for every page

1. First sentence names the subject and answers the page's implied question.
2. No "Welcome", "Discover", "In today's world" openers.
3. Exam names, specialty, or career stage appear in the first sentence.
4. No pass guarantees or empty superlatives (brand voice rule).
5. One quotable claim per section, factual and specific.

## Verify

- Read the first sentence of each page aloud. Does it answer "what is this /
  what does it cover"? If not, rewrite.
- Re-run the Magister AEO audit; the BLUF / answer-first check should rise
  above 25%.
