the relationship contract.

  not questions. Decide, then report.

  → Every deliverable ties to users → customers → revenue, never reach for its own sake.

  of my time honestly"* (chasing a logo, vendor handoffs, transfers). → The role
  must *remove* overhead from him, not add check-ins.
- **Trust already exists.** *"I have 0 concern about your ability. I believe in your
  skills."* → Do NOT re-sell competence. Sell a defined role + a revenue engine.

**Nasim's style (the value she brings — protect it):**

## 2. Strategic approach (the thesis every task serves)

**The one-line strategy:** *Convert an existing high-trust founder relationship into
a defined, revenue-owning marketing role — by proving that content tied to
per-segment business goals turns followers into paying users, using the founder's
own base + commission + scale model to earn the raise.*

**The core move: reframe "social content" → "social strategy tied to business."**
This is Nasim's wedge and the cohort's Market-to-Lead spine. Copying a viral medical
account grows followers; it does not grow *convertible* buyers. Bayan's advantage is
distinct products for distinct segments (students, residents, nurses, each with exam
tracks). So content is architected per segment, each with a job: awareness → trust →
trial → paid.

**The commercial arc (the raise ladder):** the CEO is anchored on **Plan 1 (250-300
OMR, content operator)**. The plan's job is to move him to **Plan 3/4 (650-900+ OMR,
marketing owner)** toward the **1000 OMR** goal — not by asking, but by:
1. Delivering Plan-1 output that visibly outperforms the 145 OMR/mo agency.
2. Attaching every deliverable to a funnel metric he can watch.
3. Triggering his own stated scale model: *base + 20% deal commission + scale on
   traction.* The raise is earned by traction, framed in his language.

**Brand equity is the long game (cohort backbone).** Followers are rented;
brand equity compounds. Bissan & Gassan (the CEO's own children, named) are the
equity vehicle — consistent characters + visual identity + a distinct voice that
makes Bayan recognizable without the logo. Every asset either builds that equity
or it's noise.

**Non-negotiables (CEO-set):** dark navy + gold; Omani cultural fidelity (local
people, hijab, real hospital scenarios); English-first content (Arab medical study
is in English); AI-assisted but human-owned medical content (*"AI assists with
drafting — humans own what reaches learners"*).

**Hard messaging rules (do-not-say) — brand red lines. Every asset is checked against these:**
- **No shortcuts / no "pass without studying."** The CEO enforced this himself —
  he rewrote a script so Bissan corrects Gassan: *"There are NO shortcuts, Gassan!
  That's the whole point — we help you actually LEARN the material."* Integrity-first.
  **Never "Pass Guarantee."**
- **AI is a tool, never the authority.** Approved framing: *"We use AI to make
  medical education and career pathways easier."* Never say "AI-powered" without the
  human-review caveat.
- **Market as "Gulf Licensing Exam Preparation," never "Prometric"** (OMSB runs via
  Pearson VUE). Exam names (SMLE, DHA, OMSB) may appear as *targets*; never cite
  SQUH / OMSB / SQU as the CEO's *employer*, and keep **MedResearch Academy** a
  separate brand from Bayan.
- **⚠️ The CEO's own Aug-29 brief breaks these rules** (used "Prometric,"
  "AI-powered / AI-verified," "10,000+ questions," "Pass Guarantee"). Do NOT lift
  his brief as canonical copy — reframe to comply.

## 4. Business framework (the operating model)

**Framework: EMPOWER Market-to-Lead, mapped to AARRR.** The cohort's Session 5
structure is the plan's backbone; here's how each layer runs for Bayan.

**Market-to-Lead spine (per segment):**
1. **Market** — who exactly (physician / resident / student / nurse × exam track).
2. **Message** — the one pain that segment feels (*"I stopped guessing what to
   study"*), in their words.
3. **Magnet** — the free value that earns the email/trial (exam checklist, free
   questions, QR voucher at the booth).
4. **Machine** — the content + funnel that moves them: awareness reel → trust
   content → trial → paid. Tied to a metric at each step.

**AARRR mapping (what each stage owns):**
| Stage | Bayan question | Owner metric |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | How do med professionals discover Bayan? | reach → qualified follower |
| Activation | Does a trial user get to "exam-ready" value fast? | install → trial → first value |
| Retention | Do they keep studying / stay subscribed? | trial → paid, monthly active |
| Referral | Do users bring peers (study groups spread)? | invites, shares |
| Revenue | What compounds — subscriptions, institutional? | MRR, ARPU, LTV/CAC |

**The business-model logic:** SaaS subscription; pre-revenue, investor-funded;
free-in-Oman + paid international. Marketing's job = convert product strength into
paying users to reach break-even. The CEO's pay trigger is explicit: *"the moment
we break even... things will be better for all new members."*

**The measurement gate (why §4-8 are partially TBD):** Bayan has **zero funnel data**
today. Every projection depends on installing measurement first. So the framework's
first act is *instrument the funnel* — until then, AARRR moves carry
`[TBD — funnel data]` where a real number is required, and Section 13 lists this as
the #1 open decision.

## 5. How to use this doc (for any future task)

1. **Read this + `research.md` before starting.** They're the shared brain.
2. **Check interaction style (§1)** — does the deliverable match how the CEO works
   (outcome-led, low-touch, proactive)?
3. **Check strategic approach (§2)** — does it serve the reframe (content → strategy)
   and the raise ladder?
4. **Pull facts from `research.md`** (authority order), never invent numbers.
5. **Pick the right skill** from the library (§3) for the task.
6. **Tie the output to a funnel metric** (§4). If no data exists, mark `[TBD]`.
7. **Protect brand equity** — consistent characters, voice, palette.

*If a task can't satisfy §1-4, it's the wrong task. Reconcile here first.*
