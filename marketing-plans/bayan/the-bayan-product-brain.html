Bottom line — read this if nothing else

The same fact has different values on every surface.

Bayan runs six products across a corporate site, product sites, app stores, brochures, and an exhibition booth. Today the same fact has different values on each surface. I ran the check this morning: your two main sites currently name two different companies ("MedResearch Academy" on bayan.edu.om, "Bayan AI Technologies LLC" on bayanai.tech), and your headline question count reads 4,000+ on the live site while the figure your team treats as official is 5,589 — a number that appears on neither site.

Every time this surfaces — as it did right before the Oman Health Exhibition — it costs you and your team hours to reconcile, and it puts inconsistent facts in front of buyers. And it isn't only numbers: in your words, “we have been running around cycles for missing Bayan logo” — the same root cause, a different day.

I'll fix the root cause, not the symptom: build one canonical, you-approved reference for every product's figures, pricing, positioning, taglines, and brand identity — that anyone can query in plain language, and that automatically flags any surface that disagrees with it.

Why this matters more than tidy marketing

In exam prep the stakes run deeper than tidy marketing. In a product whose entire value is being correct, a fact out of place doesn't just look sloppy — it quietly undercuts the one thing Bayan sells: trust.

"AI-powered" is already table stakes in this market; verified, consistent accuracy is the real differentiator — and a source of truth is the infrastructure that guarantees it. For a founder whose own work is held to citation-level rigor, public numbers that disagree with each other are a credibility leak; the fix should meet the same standard you'd apply to a manuscript.

What I'll build — the wedge

Three pieces, one week.

01

The Product Brain

One structured, versioned, you-approved record per product: headline figures, pricing, positioning, taglines, brand entity, live / in-development status. The single answer to "what's official?"

You already resolve conflicts by an authority rule in your head — the latest approved email beats an older brief or a stale site. The brain makes that rule explicit, shared, and re-runnable, so it doesn't live only with you.

02

Ask-the-brain

Plain-language query, in English or Arabic: "What's Bayan's official question count?" → the one approved answer, instantly. For you, Nasim, booth staff, and any future hire — so no one has to ask you.

03

Drift check

An automated pass that reads your live corporate site, product sites, and app listings and produces a drift report: every place a surface disagrees with the approved brain, listed on one page. Run it before any exhibition, launch, or brochure — you never discover the mismatch at the booth again.

Why not just a spreadsheet? A spreadsheet holds numbers; it can't read your live sites and flag the moment one drifts, and it goes stale itself the instant a site changes. The drift check does the one thing a spreadsheet can't: it catches the mismatch before your buyers do.
