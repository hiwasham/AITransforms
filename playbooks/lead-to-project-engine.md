# Lead-to-Project Engine

The repeatable motion that took **Bayan** from cold stranger to a CEO-ready
proposal, live at one URL. This is the client-agnostic skeleton — clone it for
the next lead, fill the `{{slots}}`, version-bump when you learn something.

**Honest status (read this first):**
- ✅ **Stages 1–5 are proven.** They reliably get you from stranger → credible
  diagnosis → a proposal on the decision-maker's desk, live and clickable.
  That is the hard part most freelancers never reach.
- ⚠️ **Stage 6 (the close) is unproven.** The Bayan hold is its first live test.
  Sell the next lead on a proven *path to proposal*, not a proven closer. The
  close is what we are still learning.

**EMPOWER grounding** (the house method — real cohort-4 sessions):
- Diagnosis & positioning → **Session 5, Marketing & Brand Equity**
- Revenue reframe → **Session 6, Profitability & Metrics**
- The whole sale motion (brief → proposal → objection → close) →
  **Session 7, Offering & Sales Effectiveness**

Customer-lifecycle stages this engine spans: **Market-to-Lead** (stages 1–2) →
**Lead-to-Sale** (stages 3–6).

## Stage detail

### Stage 1 — Diagnose first, never pitch cold
**Move:** Lead with evidence of *their* problem. Audit the funnel, SEO, UX, and
baseline vitals before proposing anything. You earn the right to advise by
showing you already understand their business better than their last agency did.
**Why it wins:** A diagnosis is a gift; a pitch is a cost. The lead feels helped,
not sold, and you anchor the conversation on their gap, not your service menu.
**Bayan artifacts:** `bayan-seo-audit`, `bayan-marketing-audit-nasim`,
`bayan-ux-audit`, `baseline-vitals-bayan`, `bayan-sales-diagnostic`.
**EMPOWER:** Session 5 (Marketing & Brand Equity) + Session 2 (invisible
structure of business).

### Stage 2 — Reframe marketing → revenue
**Move:** Translate every proposed activity into money. Build "one revenue
machine," not a list of marketing tasks. Split B2C and B2B so each has its own
funnel. Speak the CEO's language (revenue, CAC, LTV), not the marketer's
(reach, impressions).
**Why it wins:** CEOs fund revenue, not activity. The reframe makes your work a
P&L line, not an expense.
**Bayan artifacts:** `bayan-conversion-first-plan` (v1→v3), `BayanRevenueEngine`,
`M2L-6channels`.
**EMPOWER:** Session 6 (Profitability & Metrics).

### Stage 3 — Package as decisions, not homework
**Move:** Hand the decision-maker *choices to approve*, not a plan to study.
Each brief: here's the gap, here are 2–3 options, here's my recommendation,
your call. Nothing executes without sign-off.
**Why it wins:** A busy CEO can't act on a 40-page plan but can act on a decision.
You lower the activation energy of "yes" and keep control of scope.
**Bayan artifacts:** `ceo-decision-briefs`, `bayanCeo1pager`.
**EMPOWER:** Session 7 (Offering & Sales Effectiveness).

### Stage 4 — Propose small, measurable, tiered
**Move:** 4-week engagement with a decision gate at the end
(diagnose → activate → reactivate → decide). Two price tiers (e.g. design-only
vs design+implementation). Attach 2–3 matched case studies. End on a one-word
"yes" close.
**Why it wins:** Small + time-boxed + measurable de-risks the first yes. The
week-4 gate means they're buying a trial, not a marriage. Tiers let them choose
spend without choosing between you and nothing.
**Bayan artifacts:** `ceo-email-final`/`-v3`, `nasim/` proposals, case studies.
**EMPOWER:** Session 7 (Offering & Sales Effectiveness).

### Stage 5 — Deliver as one clickable link
**Move:** Publish everything to a single URL (Bayan used **Vercel** —
`bayanedu.vercel.app`). Short on the surface, every detail one click open.
Tell them: "read the summary boxes only, or open everything — your pace." No
attachments, no "which version," no download friction.
**Why it wins:** Proof on demand, at the CEO's own tempo. He forwards one link to
the board. You look like a product company, not a freelancer emailing zip files.
**Bayan artifacts:** the Vercel site (summary boxes + one-click depth per section).
**EMPOWER:** execution-systems hygiene (Session 9).

### Stage 6 — Handle the hold without pushing
**Move:** When the lead stalls ("internal review," "board approval"), do NOT
discount or chase. Remove the *real* blocker: give them the ROI/cost-benefit
material their internal review needs, and pin a concrete follow-up date tied to
their own timeline.
**Why it wins:** A hold is rarely about price; it's about the lead's internal
risk. Arm your champion to win the room you're not in. Pinning a date keeps the
deal alive without you nagging.
**Bayan artifact:** the board-ROI reply (bilingual) drafted for Dr. Abdullah's hold.
**EMPOWER:** Session 7 (Offering & Sales Effectiveness) — objection handling.
**Status:** ⚠️ in-flight, first live test.

## Proven language & moves (swipe file from the Bayan pitch email, 2026-10-07)

Real phrasings from the sent pitch. Reuse the *shape*, swap the specifics.

- **Give them the win (Stage 2 opener):** "You were right about conversion."
  Mirror the decision-maker's own instinct back before you add anything.
- **Hide the framework, show the number (Stage 2→3):** "You don't need to learn
  the framework. Every task traces to one lifecycle stage and to one number."
  (Lead-to-Sale / Sale-to-Delivery — the EMPOWER stages, kept behind the curtain.)
- **Turn an unknown into their decision (Stage 1 honesty):** surface the real gap
  as a question you hand back, e.g. the grandfathering question — "accounts before
  the paid cutoff keep free access; free forever, or until a date? Your call."
- **One-link delivery (Stage 5):** "One document, short on the surface, every
  detail one click open. Read the summary boxes, or open everything — your pace."
- **Reusable-system IS the pitch (Stage 4 proof):** "This is the same system we
  used for other clients, which is why the volume took days, not months. The week
  was build time, not planning time."
- **Founder-credibility emotional hook (Stage 4 close):** name the shared pain —
  "we ran an education company like yours for 11 years; we know manager-dependent
  operations" — then the vision: "after a short time we lift the whole load off you."
- **Two-tier delivery, stated plainly (Stage 4):** "We deliver the designs and
  solutions, then either implement them ourselves or hand them to your team."
- **Ask for one small yes, defer the rest (Stage 4→6):** the go now (Week-1 starts:
  segmentation + first landing pages by EOW); the pricing/free-account decisions
  "later, at your pace"; credentials via a **separate secure request**, never in
  the pitch; money reconciliation (Turkey receipts) on its own track.
