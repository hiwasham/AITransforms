# Ramit Sethi 50 Proven Email Scripts — Pattern Map

Source: user-provided PDF, imported 2026-05-17. This reference stores a transformation/index, not a full copy of the copyrighted scripts. Use it to choose a pattern and draft a fresh email in Hiwa's voice.

## Core principles extracted
- Make the email easy for a busy person to answer.
- Use one clear purpose and one clear next action.
- Match the recipient's context: assistant, mobile reader, blogger, vendor, boss, friend, etc.
- Reduce back-and-forth by proposing concrete times, options, or pre-filled context.
- Show proof of preparation before asking for help.
- Close loops after advice: report what you did and the result.
- Follow up politely, with value/context, not guilt.
- Respect power dynamics: concise, specific, and low-friction.

## Pattern families from the 50 scripts

### Set up calls and meetings with busy people
- Set up a phone call
- Set up a casual meeting while minimizing back-and-forth
- Confirm a phone call
- Send a pre-meeting update to impress a senior executive
- Set up an informational interview
- Ask for recommendations for people to talk to
- Cold email a stranger for advice
- Pitch for a consulting gig or job interview
- Reach out internally to get to know coworkers / lunch

### Get what you need when you need it
- Ask for an introduction to a specific person
- Stay in touch with business contacts without being annoying
- Get multiple questions answered in one email
- Get a quick answer the same day
- Ask someone to be your reference
- Let a contact know you offered them as a reference
- Ask for a testimonial
- Introduce two people via email
- Do customer research
- Get an internship at a top company

### Follow up and impress
- Send a thank-you email after a meeting or interview
- Keep relationships warm after the initial meeting / closing the loop
- Email someone after meeting at a conference when pursuing a relationship
- Email someone after meeting at a conference when not pursuing a relationship

### Deal with barriers
- Email a busy person's assistant
- Email someone who reads mostly on mobile
- Follow up when they did not respond the first time
- Get a response from someone who never responds
- Revive an old cold contact
- Email someone you lost touch with when you need a business favor
- Respond when rejected by a busy person

### Finesse tricky situations
- Check in after sending something and not hearing back
- Refresh someone's memory about how they know you
- Turn away an inquiry from someone you do not want to meet
- Send a reprimanding email
- Apologize for a mistake
- Ask for vacation time
- Ask a vendor to pay you

### Bloggers / creators / public people
- Get a popular blogger to review your product
- Pitch a popular blogger
- Intern for a popular blogger
- Interview a public person

### Scheduling and logistics
- Schedule appointments
- Reduce back-and-forth emails
- Write an autoresponder
- Prevent wasting time at a meeting
- Decline pointless meetings

### Personal/social
- Help a friend get a job using email
- Invite friends out
- Invite people to an event
- Write a party invitation
