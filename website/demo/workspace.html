Three roles, one record

Approves every fact the brain will say. The brain does not guess, round, or approximate — it says what Dr. Abdullah approved, word for word.

Approval happens once per fact. After that, every surface gets the same answer.

How approval works — five steps, one handoff

  1. Nasim or the team surfaces a question. "What should we say the question count is?" or "How do we describe Medad?" The question can come from anywhere — a deck, a booth script, an App Store update.
  2. Hiwa drafts a proposed answer based on what the team verified, then sends it to Dr. Abdullah for approval. The draft includes the exact wording the brain will use.
  3. Dr. Abdullah approves or corrects the fact. His approval is attached to the record — who approved it, when, and what the exact wording is.
  4. The fact goes live in the brain. From that moment, every surface that asks gets the same answer, in both languages.
  5. Nasim updates the publishable surfaces — the website, brochures, decks. The brain does not publish for her; it gives her the approved wording to use.

Nasim's lane stays Nasim's lane

The brain does not write marketing copy, design brochures, or draft Instagram posts. Nasim owns every publishable surface, and that does not change.

What the brain does

  • Answers factual questions — "How many questions does Bayan have?"
  • Checks surfaces for drift — does the live site match the approved record?
  • Provides approved wording in both languages
  • Runs pre-exhibition audits before Oman Health 2026

What Nasim still owns

  • Deciding what to publish and when
  • Writing marketing copy, captions, and CTAs
  • Designing brochures, decks, and booth layouts
  • Scheduling Instagram posts and managing the content calendar
  • Reviewing everything before it goes live

Plainly: the brain makes Nasim's job easier by giving her the right wording instantly, in both languages, and by catching drift before it reaches buyers. It does not make her job smaller. Nothing publishable moves without Nasim — that is a hard line in the build, not a courtesy.

Weekly report — what it contains and what it never will

At the end of Stage 0, Nasim and Dr. Abdullah get a weekly drift report. The report is plain, factual, and never makes claims the brain cannot back.

Weekly report contains

  • Drift count across all surfaces — how many facts are conflicting right now
  • Which surfaces are drifting and which facts they show
  • Approved vs. live comparison for each conflict
  • Time saved this week — hours not spent manually checking surfaces
  • Questions asked by the booth and sales staff

Weekly report never contains

  • Revenue or conversion claims — Bayan has no instrumented funnel
  • Outcome or pass-rate claims — the brain holds no clinical content
  • Recommendations about what to publish or when
  • Marketing copy, captions, or draft posts
  • Anything that looks like a decision Nasim or Dr. Abdullah should make

The brain reports facts. It does not decide what to do with them. See what it will never claim.
