Why one record fixes eight values

The eight values didn't appear because anyone was careless. They appeared because there was no place to look, so each surface captured the number that was true the day it was written — and then nothing told it to change.

A record with a version history means the next person writing copy doesn't have to guess which of eight numbers was the recent one. And the drift check means stale surfaces get named instead of quietly sitting there.

This is the one number to lock first. It's the figure that appears in the most places and the one most often asked for at short notice.

What the brain does with this

It does not pick. Tagline choice is brand work and that is Nasim's lane — the brain's job is to make sure she is choosing between two known options rather than discovering a third one in print.

Once you and Nasim settle it, the chosen set becomes the approved record and the other becomes a drift pattern the weekly check looks for.

How it's stored

Plain text files you own, not a system you rent

Readable without the brain

Each record is a plain file. Open it in any editor, read it without logging into anything, and keep it if you ever stop working with me.

Versioned by default

Every approval is a new version with the old value kept. “What did we say in September?” is a question with an answer.

No lock-in

There is no proprietary store to export from. The files are the record; the brain is what searches and checks them.

Retrieval

Why it finds the right fact instead of a nearby one

Asking is only useful if the answer is the approved one. Three things run on every question.

Meaning and wording together

“How many Qs do we have” and “official question count” reach the same record even though they share no words. Exact figures and product names still match literally.

Approved beats circulating

When an approved record and an old draft both mention 5,500, the approved record wins. Source rank is part of the answer, not an afterthought.

It says when it doesn't know

If nothing approved covers the question — JournalReady pricing today — the answer is “unresolved, two readings, confirm with team.” A gap is reported, not filled.

That last one is the difference between a brain you can put in front of booth staff and a chatbot you can't. An answer that might be invented is worse than no answer in a product whose value is being correct.
