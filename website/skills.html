The checks

Four checks, run on a schedule, so drift is found before a customer finds it

A check is a question the brain asks of your own surfaces, the same way every time. It produces a short list of differences and the surface each one sits on. It does not fix anything and it does not publish anything.

Check 2 — before each event

Pre-exhibition audit

Run in the week before a stand. It answers one question: if a visitor reads the brochure, then the banner, then the site on their phone, do they see the same numbers?

What it produces

  • A one-page answer sheet for booth staff, in English and Arabic
  • Every figure a visitor may ask about, with the approved value
  • A list of printed or live material that still shows an old figure
  • The phrasing to use out loud — 5,589, not “over 5,000”

What it caught last time

  • Brochure said 4,000+ questions; site said 5,000
  • Two different company names across the stand and the platform footer
  • One product tagline using AI-Powered, which the content rules exclude
  • Country reach quoted as both 30+ and 55+

Why this check exists. Printed material is the one surface that cannot be corrected after the fact. A figure that reaches a brochure wrong stays wrong for the life of the print run, so the audit runs before the order goes out, not after.

Check 4 — when something new goes live

New-surface review

A new page, listing, or deck is read once against the approved record before it joins the weekly sweep.

  1. The surface is added to the watch list. Public URL or a copy of the file. Nothing is connected to it — see how surfaces are read.
  2. Every figure on it is matched against the record. Numbers, product names, taglines, entity name, language pair.
  3. Differences come back as a short list. Each one names the fact and the line it appears on.
  4. You accept the surface or the record. Sometimes the new surface is right and the record needs updating — that is a normal outcome, not an error.
  5. It joins the weekly sweep. From then on, the same figure cannot quietly change back.

What a check never does

Inside the line

  • Reads public surfaces and files you hand over
  • Reports differences, with the surface named
  • Says plainly when a fact is unverified
  • Hands approved wording to the person who publishes

Outside the line

  • Edits a site, an app-store listing, or a brochure
  • Publishes, posts, schedules, or sends anything
  • Invents a figure to fill a gap, or rounds one to look better
  • Reviews clinical content, or stands in for clinician review
  • Reports on learners, staff, or anything a pass-rate claim would need
