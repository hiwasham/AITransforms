How to start

One week, about two calls from you, one approved record at the end

Stage 0 is deliberately small. It builds the record for the facts that are already in conflict on your live surfaces — nothing more. You approve values, Nasim keeps publishing, and at the end of the week every surface can be answered from one place.

The week What you do What you get What we need After Stage 0

The week, in order

Five steps. Two of them need you; the rest happen without you.

  1. Day 1 — Kickoff call (30 minutes)

    We walk the drift report together: the three conflicts on your live surfaces, the eight different question counts in circulation, and the JournalReady price change. You do not need to decide anything on this call. You only need to confirm the list is the right list.

  2. Days 1–3 — We build the record

    Every product, the brand and entity facts, the counts, the taglines, both languages. Each fact is written as one value with its source attached. Where a surface disagrees with another surface, both are recorded as a conflict rather than quietly resolved.

  3. Day 4 — Approval call (45 minutes)

    You get a short list of decisions, not a document to read. Each one is a single value to pick, with what each live surface currently says underneath it. Anything we could not source is marked confirm with team and waits for you rather than getting a guess.

  4. Day 5 — Nasim gets the approved wording

    Approved values and approved Arabic go to Nasim in the form she publishes from. She decides what ships, where, and when. Nothing is published by us and nothing is published by the brain.

  5. End of week — Handover

    You get the record, the drift report re-run against it, and a one-page note of what is still open. Booth and sales staff can ask it from that day.

What you actually do

Your total time in Stage 0 is about 75 minutes of calls plus a short list of approvals. That is the whole ask.

You do

  • Join a 30-minute kickoff call
  • Join a 45-minute approval call
  • Approve one value per conflicted fact
  • Say which unknowns are worth chasing and which can wait

You do not

  • Write anything
  • Read a long document before the calls
  • Learn a new tool or log in anywhere
  • Migrate a website, an app listing, or a brochure
  • Change how Nasim works

Why two calls and not one. The first call is for agreeing on what is broken; the second is for deciding. Collapsing them means you approve values before seeing how badly the surfaces disagree, which is the part that usually changes the decision.
