Trust & limits

What it can see, what it will never say, and where it stops

A company brain is only useful if you can check its boundaries. This page states them plainly enough that you can hold the build to them, and plainly enough that you can show this page to a clinician or a regulator without rewriting it first.

What it can and can't see What it will never claim Who owns the content How it handles unknowns Where it ends If you stop

What it can and can't see

The brain holds facts about Bayan's products. It does not hold the products.

It holds

  • Approved figures — 5,589 questions, 55+ countries
  • Approved product names, taglines, and one-line descriptions
  • Approved pricing and plan names, in both languages
  • Product status — live, in development, deferred
  • Which public surface currently shows which value
  • Who approved each fact, and the date

It does not hold

  • Question text, explanations, or any clinical content
  • Learner accounts, scores, attempts, or progress
  • Exam results or anything that could identify a learner
  • Patient data of any kind
  • Staff records, contracts, or payroll
  • Logins to bayan.edu.om, the app stores, or Bayan's email

Plainly: if the question bank disappeared tomorrow, the brain would still know that Bayan has 5,589 questions — and that is all it would know about them. There is no copy of the bank inside it.

Every surface the brain reads is a public one — a website anyone can open, a store listing anyone can see, a brochure you hand out. It reads them the way a visitor would. See the surfaces it watches.

Hard content locks

What it will never claim

These are not guidelines. They are refusals built into the record, so no answer can produce them even if someone asks for them directly.

It will never sayWhyWhat it says instead
A pass rate or score improvement Bayan has not measured one. A number here would be invented. The approved description of what the product is
An outcome guarantee No one can guarantee an exam result, and claiming it exposes Bayan. What the bank contains and who reviewed it
"AI-powered" on a Bayan product The content is human-written and clinician-reviewed. The phrase misdescribes it. The approved Sept-7 tagline for that product
A revenue, conversion, or growth figure There is no instrumented funnel to measure one against. Nothing — it says the figure is not tracked
A rounded version of a verified count "Over 5,000" and "nearly 6,000" are how 5,589 became eight values. The exact approved figure, every time

The same lock runs in Arabic. An approved Arabic sentence is stored as approved wording, not translated on demand, so a claim cannot slip in through a translation.

Who owns the content

Bayan owns the facts

Every record is Bayan's. The figures, the wording, the pricing, the Arabic — all of it belongs to Bayan and leaves with Bayan.

Clinicians own the medicine

The brain never reviews, edits, or substitutes for clinical review. It records that review happened; it does not perform it.

Nasim owns publishing

Nothing publishable moves without her. The brain hands her approved wording; she decides what goes out and when. How that line works.

The most important behaviour

When it doesn't know, it says so

A brain that guesses is worse than no brain, because a confident wrong answer travels further than silence. Here is what an unverified fact actually looks like when someone asks.

Asked by booth staff · Oman Health 2026
Staff

What's the JournalReady price?

Bayan brain

I can't give you a price to quote yet. Two values are on record and neither is approved:

  • $19 / $144 — the figures on the current listing
  • $4.99 / $29.99 — the proposed move, not yet approved confirm with team

Gap: this needs Dr. Abdullah's decision before anyone quotes it. Until then, say that JournalReady pricing is being updated and take the enquiry. Do not pick one.

What that buys you

  • No one invents a price at a stand in front of a customer
  • The decision lands with you as a decision, not as a task
  • The gap is visible before an exhibition, not after

How a gap closes

You approve one value. The record updates with your name and the date. Every surface still showing the old value is listed as a conflict until it is changed, and the person who owns that surface is named. The checks that find them.

Where it ends

Stage 0 is deliberately small. These are the limits of what is being delivered — not a list of things that are coming.

It does not act

  • It cannot edit a website, a listing, or a brochure
  • It cannot post, send, schedule, or publish
  • It has no login to any Bayan system
  • It does not email your team or your customers

It does not decide

  • It never picks which of two conflicting values is right
  • It never approves a fact on your behalf
  • It does not set pricing, strategy, or positioning
  • It does not review clinical accuracy

One honest limitation: the brain only knows about a surface it has been pointed at. If a tagline lives in a PDF nobody mentioned, or on a partner's site, it stays invisible until that surface is added to the list. Stage 0 covers the surfaces named on the surfaces page — adding more is a few minutes of work, but it is work someone has to ask for.

No lock-in

If you stop, you keep everything

Plain files

Every record is a readable text file in a folder you own. No database, no proprietary format, no export step. What they look like.

No subscription to cancel

Stage 0 is a one-time $500. The $200/mo drift-guard is optional and separate — stopping it leaves every approved fact intact.

Readable without us

The records are written to be read by a person. If Bayan never spoke to AITransforms again, the approved answers would still be there and still be usable.
